# Project history and enduring research question

## Enduring idea

Invisible Ledger began in December 2025 as an investigation of whether platform-mediated economic activity is evolving faster than the firm-centered, statistical and tax-reporting systems used to observe it.

Platforms create detailed private records of transactions involving drivers, merchants, creators and other participants. Published platform accounts generally recognize only the platform's own revenue. The project asks what the boundary between those records and recognized revenue teaches us about measurement and public visibility.

## Major stages

| Stage | Empirical form | Lesson retained |
|---|---|---|
| December 2025 origin | Indonesia example and ASEAN fiscal-decoupling thesis | Strong motivating problem; evidence was not yet verified |
| January 2026 paper | Large Indonesia/ASEAN wedge estimates and strong GDP/tax claims | Memorable framing, but transaction value was overinterpreted |
| July 2026 NTA version | Corrected ratios and sources; causal Malaysia claim withdrawn | Reporting/visibility framing became more defensible |
| August 2026 thesis build | Historical, quarterly, policy, ASEAN and investor modules | More evidence, but incompatible samples were presented too closely |
| September scope review | Indonesia FY2023 source-led comparison | Geography and derivations clarified; temporal coverage became too narrow |
| Current expansion | Additional issuers, years, quarters and source reconciliation | Build the longitudinal sample from source eligibility, not a promised row count |

The core idea is not the ecosystem-ratio arithmetic. The contribution sought is a reproducible measurement protocol and an analysis of how geography, business perimeter, incentives, accounting definitions and missing participant-side data affect what can be inferred from platform disclosures.
