## Layout vocabulary

Drawn from your decks. Every slide uses one of these containers:

| Code | Container | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | Title slide | Opening |
| **S** | Statement slide: one large statement, a few supporting lines, lots of air | Preface; breathing room |
| **2C-TV** | Two columns: labelled text blocks left, visual right (about 55/45) | Concept plus evidence |
| **2C-TT** | Two columns of labelled text blocks | Comparisons; parallel ideas |
| **FV** | Full-width visual, subtitle carries the finding, one or two lines beneath | The key evidence chart |
| **TB** | Table-led: a compact table as the centrepiece, interpretation block beside or beneath | Numeric results |
| **DG** | Diagram-led: the diagram *is* the slide | Chains and structure |
| **QA** | Q&A blocks | Robustness |

**Rules on every slide:**
- **Title** names the step. **Subtitle** (in quotes) states the finding, so a reader who sees only the titles gets the whole argument.
- **Labelled blocks** in capitals (THE TEST: / FINDING: / KEY POINT:) with → lines.
- **Only one large number per slide.** The rest are small.
- **Sub-context lines** (small, bottom or a callout) carry supporting results instead of spending a slide on them.
- **Citations** sit inline, small.

**Visuals** (equations, tables and diagrams count) are rationed and varied in type and position.

## MOVEMENT II — How experts would answer, what's missing, what we ask

### Slide 6 — What the literature can and cannot answer · 2C-TT
**Subtitle:** *"Experts explain why the numbers differ, not when the difference misleads."*

**LEFT: WHAT WE KNOW**
- **PLATFORM ECONOMICS** (Rochet & Tirole, 2003; Hagiu & Wright, 2015):
  → Platforms price through fees and subsidies across both sides of the market
  → Revenue *need not* track the commerce a platform coordinates
- **ACCOUNTING** (IFRS 15; Bowen, Davis & Rajgopal, 2002):
  → Principal/agent rules and incentives decide how much of a sale becomes revenue
- **KPI RESEARCH** (Givoly et al., 2019):
  → A reported measure loses information when its basis of computation changes
- **MEASUREMENT** (Henderson, Storeygard & Weil, 2012; Ahmad & Schreyer, 2016):
  → An indicator must be *tested* against what it stands in for
  → National accounts separate a platform's service from the commerce it carries

**RIGHT: WHAT'S MISSING**
- **UNANSWERED:**
  → *When* does revenue stop representing platform commerce?
  → Does platform data represent *national* growth?
- **WHY IT'S HARD:**
  → It needs both numbers from the same company, over time, plus a benchmark of normal drift
  → Indonesia adds local issuers reporting both, *and* national surveys that split online sales by channel
- **THE STUDY'S PLACE:**
  → Tests the substitution the literature explains but doesn't measure


**▸ BREAKDOWN:** Positions the study: each field explains a piece, none answers *when* revenue misleads, or whether platforms represent the nation. It turns the case into a research gap and establishes the study's worth.

**▸ HANDOFF:** "That gap becomes the research question, in two links." → 7

**▸ ASSESSMENT:** Important for credibility; it shows you know the field. Risk: it's the densest text slide; speak to the right column and let the left sit as reference. Likely question: 'what about papers on GMV and returns?' Answer honestly: KPI work exists (Givoly et al.); the specific substitution test is what's new.

### Slide 8 — From question to measurement · 2C-TV
**Subtitle:** *"The size of the gap isn't the test. Whether it stays stable is."*

**LEFT: labelled blocks**
- **THE LOGIC:**
  → Revenue = share kept × transaction value
  → If the share holds steady, revenue and transactions grow together
  → If the share moves, revenue tells a different growth story
- **WHAT THE DATA MUST SHOW:**
  → H1: is Indonesia's divergence larger than normal?
  → H2: does national growth come mainly from more participating businesses?

**RIGHT: equations as the visual (large, stacked)**
- W = V − R · *invisible wedge (level)*
- E = W / R = 1/m − 1 · *Ecosystem Ratio (m = R/V, the share kept)*
- **D = g(R) − g(V)** · ***growth divergence: the central test*** *(largest)*

**Sub-context:** D ≈ 0 → revenue approximately preserves the transaction-growth signal · |D| large → the share kept changed enough to alter the growth story


**▸ BREAKDOWN:** Converts the question into measurement: the logic (share kept × transactions) then the equations, with D as the test. Bridges logic into empirics and closes Movement II.

**▸ HANDOFF:** "What data can actually run this test?" → 9

**▸ ASSESSMENT:** Well placed: equations arrive only after the audience knows why they matter. Likely questions: 'what is m?' (the share kept) and 'why D, not E?' (E is a level; D tracks change). Spoken line: "W and E tell me whether the levels differ. D tells me whether that difference changes the growth conclusion." If pressed on precision: since R = m × V, D reflects a change in m, and D ≈ g(m) only for small changes. Derivation in backup B7. Never say "safe proxy".

## MOVEMENT IV — What it means

### Slide 15 — Robustness and boundaries · QA
**Subtitle:** *"Strong conclusions, drawn inside clear limits."*

**LEFT: Q&A**
- **Q: Is it one outlier?**
  A: No. Without the largest case (Blibli 2022–23, +430.61 pp), the test still gives p = 0.006.
- **Q: Aren't a firm's years dependent?**
  A: Yes, so the test is repeated with one median per firm (p = 0.024).
- **Q: Do the constructed figures drive it?**
  A: No. Grab and Shopee are excluded from H1.
- **Q: Is it causal?**
  A: No. This is a measurement and comparability study.

**RIGHT: BOUNDARIES**
- **DOES NOT IMPLY:** hidden GDP · unpaid tax · investor mispricing · error in any one indicator
- **LIMITS:**
  → Three firms disclose both numbers
  → A purposive benchmark
