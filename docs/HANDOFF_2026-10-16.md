## Current thesis spine

The thesis is now best understood as an economic-measurement thesis on platform-mediated activity in Indonesia.

The nested structure is:

1. platform revenue sits inside platform commerce / transaction value;
2. platform commerce sits inside all online commerce;
3. H1 tests whether transaction value and platform revenue grow proportionally when the take rate is stable;
4. H2 tests whether national e-commerce growth mainly comes from more businesses selling online;
5. the registered entry test asks whether the published rise in online-seller counts is actually explained by first-time entry.

The central semantic claim is not that transaction value minus revenue is missing GDP, hidden income, or unpaid tax. The central issue is whether observable proxies remain stable representations of the underlying activity.

## Main empirical state from the 15 October draft

### Platform side / H1

- Main comparison: five Indonesian/regional platform series versus 27 foreign platforms.
- Median annual log take-rate change: about 0.20 versus 0.06; Mann-Whitney p = .004.
- Shuffle/randomization p = .022.
- Growth-divergence comparison: about 27.0 percentage points versus 6.5; p = .005.
- Leave-one-platform-out p values remain approximately .003-.016.
- Quarterly pairs do not reject (p = .19), so the finding should be bounded mainly to the 2021-2024 / especially 2022-2024 repricing period rather than treated as a permanent platform characteristic.
- Indonesian/regional starting take rates are much thinner than the foreign comparison, so similar absolute percentage-point fee moves generate much larger proportional revenue changes.

### Wedge

For Tokopedia, Shopee Indonesia and Grab Indonesia in 2023, the draft totals roughly:

- transaction value: US$43.23bn;
- platform revenue: US$3.16bn;
- transaction-value-minus-revenue wedge: US$40.07bn;
- ecosystem ratio: about 12.7.

The wedge is a measurement transformation only. It is not GDP, taxable income, undeclared income or unpaid tax.

### Market-value extension

- Main sample: 28 listed platforms, 138 platform-years.
- Transaction value is positively associated with market value (beta about 0.6, p = .002).
- The prediction that markets value take-rate growth less than transaction growth fails in the main sample; coefficient-gap p is about .20.
- Keep that failed prediction. Do not promote the mixed segment/whole-company alternative run as the main result.

### BPS / H2

- Published 2022-2023 online-sales-value growth: about 40.6% / 41% in rupiah terms.
- Published seller-count growth: about 27.4%.
- On the published series, the extensive margin contributes about 71% of log sales-value growth, so H2 holds.
- BPS microdata validation: weighted totals for the available rounds closely reproduce BPS published totals.

### Registered entry test

- To explain 27.4% seller-count growth purely through entry with no exits, at least 21.5% of current sellers would need to have begun selling online in 2023.
- The survey gives only about 13-13.45%.
- Therefore the registered entry test fails.
- Exploratory accounting estimates about 306,000 unexplained sellers under a no-exit interpretation and about 458,000 under a 5% annual-exit interpretation.
- Returning sellers cannot be ruled out because the survey rounds cannot link the same business across years.

### New coverage-adjusted H2 scenarios

See `docs/analysis/H2_COVERAGE_ADJUSTED_SCENARIOS_2026-10-16.md`.

- Published count: extensive-margin share about 71.1% -> H2 holds.
- Remove 306,000 unexplained sellers: about 46.5% -> H2 no longer meets its >50% rule.
- Remove 458,000 unexplained sellers: about 33.6% -> H2 again does not meet the rule.

These are exploratory coverage scenarios, not corrected or "true" BPS counts. The important interpretation is that the substantive conclusion changes when the measurement link between counted sellers and genuine entry changes.

## Thesis versus publication architecture

Do not force the full thesis directly into a journal manuscript.

The thesis should remain the broad umbrella research programme.

The likely main publication paper should be narrower: when digital-economy proxies stop tracking the activity they are supposed to represent. The platform H1 result and the BPS entry/coverage result are the strongest paired demonstrations of that idea. Tax/regulation can then become an implication rather than the main empirical spine.

A separate digital-tax paper can build on this by studying the next link: how unstable or partial observable measures become statutory, reported and collectible tax bases. Keep those papers semantically connected but empirically distinct.
