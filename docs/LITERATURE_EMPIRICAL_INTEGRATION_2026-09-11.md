# Invisible Ledger: literature–empirical integration

**Status:** manuscript architecture based on the certified empirical modules available on 11 September 2026. This document connects the evidence to the closest literature, identifies the paper's defensible contribution, and specifies a section-by-section reconstruction plan. It does not pool the evidence modules or convert descriptive results into causal claims.

## Technical summary

The rebuilt paper should ask:

> How does the interpretation of Indonesia's digital-commercial transformation change when activity is observed through platform transaction measures, issuer revenue, business participation and recordkeeping, official statistics, and tax-reporting institutions?

The answer supported by the current evidence is:

> These ledgers measure different economic objects and can move differently. Issuer transaction activity and recognized revenue sometimes provide opposing growth signals; BPS estimates show that recent Indonesian e-commerce growth was associated mainly with more participating businesses and faster non-marketplace activity; marketplace users exhibit stronger conventional recordkeeping and lower cash reliance in published business-level comparisons; and ASEAN and global evidence show that recurrence does not imply common definitions, business models, or institutions.

This is a measurement and information-boundary contribution. It does not estimate missing GDP, undeclared participant income, unpaid tax, or a causal marketplace effect.

The novelty is not the arithmetic identity that transaction value exceeds platform revenue. The contribution is the source-auditable reconciliation of several observational systems within one country setting, showing which economic conclusions change when the ledgers are aligned by period, geography, business perimeter, and definition.

## Closest literature and the role of this study

### 1. Platform economics explains why transaction activity and revenue need not move together

Rochet and Tirole (2003) show that a two-sided platform chooses a price structure across participant groups, not merely one aggregate price. Hagiu and Wright (2015) distinguish multi-sided platforms from vertically integrated and reseller models. This literature predicts that commissions, subsidies, advertising, logistics, subscriptions, and other monetization channels may change without proportional changes in facilitated transaction value.^1,2

**Empirical connection.** The Indonesia issuer module contains 13 direct candidate periods and nine direct-candidate transitions. Three transitions have opposite transaction/revenue growth signs. The all-tier inventory contains 17 levels and 12 transitions, but includes conditional country reconstructions. These are diagnostics of non-equivalence, not estimates of a common platform effect.

**What this study adds.** Rather than estimating network effects or optimal prices, it documents how platform business-model choices affect the empirical signals available to researchers examining Indonesia. The strongest contribution is identifying when transaction and revenue measures cease to be interchangeable and then tracing the reason where source components permit.

### 2. Revenue recognition and KPI disclosure explain the issuer-accounting boundary

IFRS 15 distinguishes a principal that controls the specified good or service before transfer from an agent that arranges for another party to provide it. The distinction affects whether gross consideration or a net amount is recognized as revenue. The SEC's KPI guidance stresses that registrants should define material operating metrics, explain their calculation, disclose material assumptions, and describe changes in methodology or presentation.^3,4

Financial-statement comparability research shows why consistent accounting functions matter to users of financial information. De Franco, Kothari, and Verdi (2011) link greater comparability to analyst following and forecast properties. That paper does not study GMV, Indonesian platforms, or digital-economy measurement, but it provides a finance/accounting rationale for examining whether ostensibly similar issuer metrics map comparable economic events into accounting numbers.^5

**Empirical connection.** Tokopedia's FY2022–FY2023 reconciliation shows transaction value falling approximately 8.90% while third-party net segment revenue rises approximately 53.20%. Of the increase in net revenue, 39.4% is arithmetically associated with higher gross revenue and 60.6% with reduced customer incentives. Grab, Blibli, Bukalapak, and the global issuer sample add cases involving principal/agent presentation, business mix, travel inclusion, overseas scope, and reporting-vintage changes.

**What this study adds.** It converts comparability from a generic limitation into an empirical object: which growth conclusion would a reader reach from transaction value, gross revenue, net revenue, or a later restated/comparable basis?

### 3. National-accounting standards establish that the transaction and intermediation service are different objects

The decisive conceptual anchor is the emerging statistical treatment of digital intermediation platforms. The OECD's Digital Supply and Use Table framework separates the nature of the transaction (the “how”), the product (the “what”), and the producing industry (the “who”). It distinguishes digitally ordered transactions made directly with a producer from those made through a digital intermediation platform.^6

The 2025 System of National Accounts and the joint Handbook on Measuring Digital Trade distinguish the sale between buyer and seller from the intermediation service supplied by the platform. The platform's fee is not the same economic object as the value of the intermediated product.^7,8 This supports the paper's core distinction while also invalidating the earlier shortcut of treating transaction value minus platform revenue as missing GDP.

Ahmad and Schreyer (2016) and the IMF (2018) show that digitalization creates practical measurement problems involving classification, cross-border activity, price measurement, data lags, and platform fees, while cautioning against assuming that the conceptual GDP boundary has simply failed. Byrne, Fernald, and Reinsdorf (2016) likewise show why digital mismeasurement should not be invoked as a universal explanation for macroeconomic puzzles.^9,10,11

**Empirical connection.** BPS reports both the broader value of Indonesian e-commerce and its sales-channel composition. The issuer data report selected platforms' facilitated activity and corporate revenue. These sources should not reconcile one-to-one because they correspond to different actors and measurement perimeters.

**What this study adds.** It provides a concrete Indonesia case showing how the statistical distinction operates in actual issuer and business data. The claim is not that national accounts omit the entire difference; it is that conventional firm-centered and marketplace-centered views do not visibly communicate all dimensions of the transformation to public users.

### 4. Small-business recordkeeping literature explains why participant-side visibility matters

McKenzie and Woodruff (2017) treat recordkeeping and financial planning as measurable business practices and show that better practices predict stronger firm outcomes across developing-country microenterprises. La Porta and Shleifer (2014) and Ulyssea (2018) situate weak formal organization and informality within development, while warning against treating all small or informal firms as one homogeneous category.^12,13,14

Digital-footprint research shows that electronically generated information can contain economically useful signals even when conventional records are thin. Berg, Burg, Gombović, and Puri (2020), for example, find that simple digital-footprint variables can carry credit-relevant information comparable to traditional bureau scores. This is not evidence about Indonesian tax compliance, but it supports a broader distinction between digital traceability and conventional financial documentation.^15

**Empirical connection.** BPS's published business-level analysis covers 39,736 businesses: 6,148 marketplace users and 33,588 non-marketplace users. Complete financial-statement ownership is 28.63% among marketplace users and 12.25% among non-users. Cash use is 37.74% versus 83.29%. BPS's multivariate model reports an odds ratio of 1.8463 for the association between financial-statement ownership and marketplace use.

**What this study adds.** It places these participant-side differences beside issuer and national activity measures. Marketplace participation appears associated with stronger conventional records and less cash reliance, but selection remains a strong alternative explanation. Digital visibility and formal recordkeeping overlap without being equivalent.

### 5. Third-party-information research explains the potential administrative bridge

Kleven et al. (2011), Pomeranz (2015), and Slemrod (2019) establish that third-party information and verifiable transaction chains are central to tax compliance and enforcement. A recent platform-specific study of U.S. gig workers reports that the loss of third-party information returns reduced reported profits, providing direct evidence that platform reporting can change tax declarations in that setting.^16,17,18,19

The OECD's 2020 Model Rules require covered platforms to collect and report seller identity and consideration information for specified activities. Indonesia's PMK 37/2025 similarly creates a legal architecture under which designated marketplaces collect seller identifiers and perform defined withholding and reporting roles.^20,21

**Empirical connection.** The repository documents the Indonesian legal reporting bridge and designated marketplaces. It does not yet establish operating coverage, successful record matching, compliance effects, or the visibility of non-marketplace channels.

As market context, the four marketplaces designated by DJP in July 2026—Blibli, Shopee Indonesia, Tokopedia, and Lazada—correspond to 83% of the 2023 Indonesian platform e-commerce GMV shares reported by Katadata from Momentum Works. This is a cross-year coverage diagnostic, not evidence that 83% of merchants, tax liabilities, or 2026 transactions were covered in practice. The designation was subsequently postponed through 31 October 2026, so the current evidence establishes institutional design rather than a completed policy effect.

**What this study adds.** It identifies the exact information bridge required to move from public measurement divergence to a test of administrative visibility. The current data support the institutional design, not its causal effectiveness.

### 6. Measurement revision and cross-country heterogeneity bound the external claim

The OECD digital-measurement framework permits experimental outputs when data are available and recognizes that digitalization is multidimensional. This supports retaining publication vintages and defining uncertainty rather than silently overwriting old estimates.^6

**Empirical connection.** The ASEAN module retains 450 publication-vintage rows and derives 42 latest-vintage country-years across six countries. The global issuer module contains 48 issuer-years and 29 clean-screened transitions across eight platform businesses. Both show recurrence, but neither is a representative pooled sample.

**What this study adds.** ASEAN demonstrates that regional expansion occurs under heterogeneous tax, regulatory, and platform structures; the global issuer evidence demonstrates that transaction/revenue divergence recurs across materially different platform models. These modules support external relevance without validating Indonesia-specific allocations.

## Proposed manuscript architecture

### 1. Introduction: one economy, several observable ledgers

Open with the empirical puzzle rather than the old residual:

> Indonesia's digital commerce is visible in platform transaction records, corporate accounts, business surveys, payment practices, and tax rules, but those records do not measure the same economic object and can imply different accounts of growth.

The introduction should state four findings:

1. Transaction activity and recognized revenue diverge within longitudinal issuer series.
2. Tokopedia shows how incentives and gross/net revenue contribute to an opposite-sign movement.
3. BPS data show that 2023–2024 growth was participation-intensive and concentrated in the non-marketplace component.
4. Marketplace users have stronger conventional records, but the available evidence identifies association rather than causal formalization.

End with three contributions: an Indonesia measurement reconciliation, a mechanism analysis of issuer divergence, and a cross-ledger visibility framework linked to participant and administrative evidence.

### 2. Institutional setting: how Indonesian digital commerce is recorded

Describe separately:

- platform operating metrics and issuer accounts;
- BPS e-commerce surveys and sales-media measures;
- merchant financial records and payment practices;
- PMK 37/2025 seller-information, withholding, and reporting design.

This section should identify the observer attached to each ledger. It should not imply that BPS, marketplaces, payment providers, and tax authorities share a common record-level database.

### 3. Conceptual framework: transaction, intermediation service, revenue, and value added

Use the SNA/Digital SUT distinction as the foundation:

```text
buyer pays for product/service  <------>  seller supplies product/service
                \                            /
                 \                          /
                  platform supplies intermediation
                       and recognizes its revenue
```

Define transaction value, selected issuer revenue, merchant receipts, intermediation fees, incentives, and value added. Show that `(V-R)/R = 1/t - 1` is a descriptive transformation and not the theoretical contribution.

State hypotheses as bounded propositions:

- H1: transaction and revenue growth need not coincide within stable issuer perimeters;
- H2: changes in monetization, incentives, and reporting perimeter explain part of the divergence;
- H3: aggregate e-commerce growth can occur through business participation and non-marketplace channels not summarized by issuer revenue;
- H4: marketplace participation is associated with different recordkeeping and payment characteristics;
- H5: legal reporting architecture creates a potential information bridge whose implementation effects remain an open test.

### 4. Data and research design: linked questions, separate samples

Present modules rather than one artificial N:

| Module | Unit | Coverage | Analytical role |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Indonesia issuer | issuer/segment-year and transition | 13 direct periods; 9 transitions | main longitudinal evidence |
| Revenue components | issuer-period component reconciliation | source-dependent | mechanism evidence |
| BPS national | national year and channel | 2022–2024 | growth anatomy |
| BPS business | published business-level comparison | 39,736 businesses | participant-side evidence |
| BPS province | province-year | 74 complete province-years | negative/ecological robustness |
| ASEAN | country-year and publication vintage | 42 canonical country-years | regional corroboration |
| Global issuer | issuer-year and transition | 48 issuer-years; 29 clean transitions | business-model corroboration |
| Quarterly | issuer/segment-quarter | 47 quarters | supporting frequency check |

Specify directness tiers, admission rules, restatement treatment, transition construction, and why the modules are not pooled.

### 5. Results I: transaction activity and revenue tell different issuer stories

Lead with the direct-candidate transition results, not the FY2023 aggregate. Report the number and direction of transitions and show issuer-specific trajectories. Treat the 42.94 percentage-point median absolute divergence as a sample diagnostic, not an estimated population effect.

Use the conditional Grab and Shopee country reconstructions only as sensitivity. Keep the FY2023 three-case residual as a clearly labelled historical illustration or appendix calculation.

### 6. Results II: mechanisms behind divergence

Make Tokopedia the central accounting reconciliation. Then organize other issuer evidence by mechanism:

- incentives;
- gross/net and principal/agent basis;
- monetization mix;
- acquisition or deconsolidation;
- segment and geographic perimeter;
- publication vintage.

The objective is to explain why the observed signal changes, not to rank platforms by take rate.

### 7. Results III: broader Indonesian activity and participant records

Present the certified BPS decomposition:

- total e-commerce value: +17.08% in 2023–2024;
- estimated e-commerce businesses: +15.31%;
- implied nominal value per business: +1.54%;
- marketplace component: +1.45%;
- non-marketplace component: +20.57%.

Then present the BPS business-level marketplace comparison. Use the province analysis as a negative result showing that ecological correlations do not recover the business-level relationship.

### 8. Results IV: institutional visibility and reporting architecture

Map which actors can directly observe each record and which linkages are established:

| Ledger | Direct observer | Publicly observed | Administrative linkage established? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform transaction | platform | partly, through KPIs | legal framework exists; operating coverage unresolved |
| Issuer revenue | issuer, investors | yes | not a seller-income measure |
| Merchant financial record | merchant; BPS through survey | aggregate statistics | tax-record linkage unresolved |
| Payment trace | provider/regulator | generally no | linked research data unavailable |
| BPS estimate | BPS | aggregate/province publications | exact overlap with platform flows unresolved |
| Tax record | taxpayer and tax authority | legal design public | compliance effect unresolved |

This section turns “visibility” into a defined institutional object rather than a metaphor.

### 9. ASEAN and global corroboration: recurrence without pooling

ASEAN should show separate country histories, market-structure heterogeneity, and vintage revisions. Global issuers should show that activity/revenue divergence recurs across business models. Neither module should be described as validating the Indonesia sample.

### 10. Discussion: what the different ledgers change

The discussion should distinguish three meanings of “not visible”:

1. not visible in issuer revenue;
2. not separately visible to users of aggregate public statistics;
3. not shown to be linked at participant level across platform, statistical, payment, and tax systems.

The current evidence establishes the first, partly documents the second, and specifies—but does not prove—the third.

### 11. Conclusion

The conclusion should be:

> Indonesia's digital transformation is not absent from economic records, but it is distributed across records with different units, scopes, and institutional owners. Reconstructing those ledgers changes the observed scale, direction, and composition of growth. Platform revenue is therefore an incomplete proxy for coordinated commercial activity, while the existence of digital transaction records does not by itself establish statistical or tax-administrative linkage.

## Interpretation and robustness rules

1. Never add annual, quarterly, province, country, issuer, source-vintage, or business-level counts into one N.
2. Compare growth only within stable or explicitly reconciled issuer series.
3. Treat scope-break transitions separately from clean transitions.
4. Prefer direct amounts to quantities reconstructed from rounded shares.
5. Keep conditional country allocations out of the direct main sample.
6. Describe BPS marketplace comparisons as associations; selection into marketplaces is not resolved.
7. Do not interpret non-marketplace commerce as unrecorded or untaxed.
8. Do not interpret revenue–transaction divergence as welfare, productivity, value added, or tax loss.
9. Use ASEAN and global evidence to test recurrence and heterogeneity, not to manufacture a larger Indonesia sample.
10. Report negative results, including the unstable province-level relationship.

## Contribution statement

The defensible contribution is:

> This study builds a source-auditable, multi-ledger account of Indonesia's digital commerce. It shows that facilitated transaction activity, issuer revenue, business participation, financial recordkeeping, and official market estimates can evolve differently because they observe different actors and economic objects. By aligning periods, definitions, business perimeters, and publication vintages, the study identifies which apparent divergences reflect monetization and accounting mechanisms, which reflect channel composition, and which require participant-level institutional linkage before stronger statistical or tax conclusions can be made.

This statement is narrower than “GDP misses the digital economy,” but stronger than “GMV exceeds revenue.” It retains the original ambition by demonstrating precisely where a narrow public view underdescribes digital-commercial transformation and what additional linkage would be required to quantify what remains outside that view.
