# Invisible Ledger - research preview blueprint

**Status:** narrative blueprint for the thesis proposal and later manuscript. It is downstream of `docs/CANONICAL_EMPIRICAL_RESULTS_2026-09-12.md` and does not choose the final advisor-approved issuer sample.

## 1. Big motivating question

> **If digital commercial activity has become enormous, where does that growth actually show up in the economy - and why can its apparent impact look so different depending on what is measured?**

This question is intentionally larger than the thesis can answer completely. The project does not claim to solve national-accounting, productivity, welfare, and tax measurement at once. Instead, it follows one tractable part of the puzzle: how digitally mediated commercial activity is translated into corporate, business, statistical, payment, and administrative records.

## 2. Research journey

### Stage A - The original platform boundary

The project begins with its original root: platform-mediated activity and platform-recognized revenue are related but different economic objects. The first empirical question is not whether transaction value exceeds revenue, but whether the two measures imply the same account of change.

The longitudinal issuer evidence shows they need not. Some candidate transitions even reverse direction. This establishes the first translation problem:

> growth in platform-mediated commerce does not translate mechanically into growth in the platform company's recognized revenue.

### Stage B - Why does the translation differ?

Mechanism reconciliations examine monetization, customer incentives, gross/net presentation, principal-agent treatment, business mix, acquisition/deconsolidation, and reporting perimeter.

The strongest current example is Tokopedia FY2022->FY2023: transaction activity falls while selected net revenue rises, with the arithmetic revenue increase largely associated with lower customer incentives plus higher gross revenue.

The point is not Tokopedia itself. It is that cross-record disagreement can reveal economic structure rather than merely generate a measurement warning.

### Stage C - Maybe this is only corporate accounting

The study then moves to official BPS evidence. If the underlying digital transformation is broader than the platform company, where else does growth appear?

BPS shows that recent national e-commerce growth is closely accompanied by growth in the estimated number of participating businesses and that the marketplace and non-marketplace components follow sharply different trajectories.

This produces the second translation result:

> digital-commercial growth can manifest through broader participation and channel composition that issuer accounts do not summarize.

### Stage D - Are the fastest-growing parts also the easiest to observe?

BPS's published business-level evidence shows marketplace users are associated with stronger conventional financial records and much lower cash reliance than non-marketplace users. Yet the latest measured national channel growth is overwhelmingly concentrated in the non-marketplace component.

This creates a central tension:

> the comparatively more structured and conventionally legible channel is not necessarily the channel where the latest measured growth occurs.

The thesis must keep this descriptive. It does not establish that marketplace participation causes formalization or that non-marketplace activity is hidden/untaxed.

### Stage E - Doesn't digital payment data solve the visibility problem?

Bank Indonesia provides an independent test. Payment traces are abundant and grow rapidly, but QRIS, electronic-money shopping, mobile-banking purchases, BPS e-commerce, and issuer measures observe different actions and populations.

This establishes that digital trace abundance does not remove the need to understand economic scope.

### Stage F - When does a trace become institutionally usable?

PMK 37/2025 supplies the final institutional question. A platform may hold transaction and seller information, but public administration requires identity, legally defined turnover, reporting obligations, transmission, matching, and operational use.

The current evidence establishes a legal bridge, not post-implementation effectiveness.

## 3. Invisible Ledger concept

The title should emerge from the investigation rather than be assumed at the start.

> **The Invisible Ledger is the fuller economic account that becomes recoverable when the relationships, disagreements, and boundaries among partial records are reconciled.**

It is not:
- transaction value minus platform revenue;
- missing GDP;
- undeclared income;
- unpaid tax;
- a proposed centralized database.

The method is reconciliation of interpretation, not forced consolidation of data.

## 4. Three formal research questions

### RQ1 - Firm translation

How does growth in digitally mediated commercial activity translate into recognized platform-company revenue, and what mechanisms explain divergence when the two measures do not move together?

### RQ2 - Broader economic translation

Where else does digital-commercial growth appear in business participation, sales channels, and payment activity, and how does this alter the interpretation of growth?

### RQ3 - Institutional translation

Under what conditions do digital traces become statistically or administratively usable economic information?

## 6. Stakes

The thesis should not claim harm simply because records differ. Stakes arise when the most visible record becomes a proxy for a wider economic process.

Potential decision-relevant consequences supported at the prerequisite level include:

- issuer revenue can imply a different growth diagnosis from underlying transaction activity;
- marketplace-centered evidence can mislocate where national e-commerce growth is occurring;
- aggregate value alone can conceal whether expansion reflects broader participation or larger average activity;
- payment growth can be mistaken for equivalent commerce growth;
- private digital traceability can be mistaken for public or administrative visibility.

The paper establishes these measurement prerequisites. It does not yet estimate investor losses, welfare loss, tax evasion, or policy failure.
