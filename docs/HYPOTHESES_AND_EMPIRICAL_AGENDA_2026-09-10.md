## Proposed central research question

How does the economic picture of Indonesia's digital commerce change depending
on whether activity is observed through platform transaction measures,
corporate revenue, merchant records, market estimates, official statistics, or
tax-administrative rules, and where do those ledgers fail to connect?

This preserves the original *Invisible Ledger* ambition while replacing an
unsupported national residual with testable questions about measurement,
coverage, comparability, and institutional linkage.

## Proposed substantive answer

The working interpretation is:

> Digitalization does not necessarily make economic activity unrecorded. It
> creates detailed records in multiple private and public systems, but those
> systems measure different objects and are not automatically comparable or
> linkable. A firm-revenue view can therefore underdescribe the scale,
> participation, or direction of platform-coordinated commerce even when the
> underlying activity exists in another ledger.

The current evidence supports the premise that ledgers differ. It does not yet
establish the institutional non-linkage or quantify its national consequence.

## Hypothesis 1: corporate revenue and coordinated commercial activity can give different longitudinal signals

### Claim to test

Within a stable issuer/business perimeter, growth in the platform's transaction
measure and growth in recognized revenue frequently differ enough to change the
interpretation of commercial expansion.

### Existing support

The global corroboration module already finds a median absolute growth
difference of 7.10 percentage points among twenty-nine clean-screened
transitions and four opposite-sign cases.

### Next analysis

Apply a consistent longitudinal decomposition to every admissible Indonesia
issuer/segment series:

- transaction growth;
- recognized-revenue growth;
- monetization-rate change;
- gross revenue, incentives, and net revenue where disclosed;
- reporting, acquisition, and business-model breaks;
- issuer-provided reported versus comparable bases.

Classify each eligible transition as:

- activity up / revenue up;
- activity up / revenue down;
- activity down / revenue up;
- activity down / revenue down.

### Falsification or weakening condition

If the Indonesia series move nearly one-for-one after consistent definitions
and perimeter breaks are removed, then the global divergence is corroborating
context but not a central Indonesia finding.

### Required data

- final Indonesia annual admission census;
- resolved Blibli publication-vintage rule;
- explicit treatment of Blibli travel and Bukalapak overseas scope;
- period-matched Tokopedia observations;
- source-reported revenue components where available.

## Hypothesis 2: aggregate Indonesian digital-commerce growth is partly an extensive-margin phenomenon

### Claim to test

Growth in Indonesian e-commerce reflects expansion in the number and geographic
distribution of participating businesses, not only higher transaction value
per incumbent business.

### Existing support

The current BPS aggregate decomposition shows 2023–2024 estimated-business
growth of 15.31% and implied nominal transaction-value-per-business growth of
1.54%.

### Next analysis

- extend the decomposition across every comparable BPS publication year;
- separate nominal scale growth from business-count growth;
- map province contributions to changes in estimated business participation;
- test whether growth is geographically concentrated or broad-based;
- identify sector and firm-size composition where published tables permit.

### Falsification or weakening condition

If questionnaire populations, reference years, or weights are not comparable,
the result must remain a single-period descriptive decomposition. If value per
business dominates in other comparable periods, the extensive-margin result
must be reported as period-specific.

### Required data

- verified questionnaires and reference periods;
- national and province estimates with denominators and weights;
- licensed microdata only if access is approved and harmonization is feasible.

## Hypothesis 3: platform participation and conventional financial recordkeeping are not equivalent forms of visibility

### Claim to test

Businesses can participate in digitally traceable marketplace transactions
without necessarily maintaining conventional financial statements, creating a
gap between platform-level traceability and merchant-side recordkeeping.

### Existing support

BPS publishes separate marketplace-participation and financial-report-ownership
indicators. Their separate existence is not proof of their joint relationship.

### Preferred empirical test

Using authorized BPS business microdata or a published cross-tabulation:

- estimate financial-report ownership by marketplace-use status;
- apply survey weights and design information;
- report differences by business size, sector, province, age, and survey wave;
- treat waves as repeated cross-sections unless BPS documents a valid
  longitudinal enterprise key.

### Falsification or weakening condition

If marketplace users have equal or stronger recordkeeping after transparent
conditioning, the thesis should report that digital participation may improve
conventional visibility rather than presume a gap. If compatible joint data
cannot be obtained, the relationship remains untested.

### Required data

- BPS microdata access or official joint tables;
- questionnaire wording, skip patterns, weights, strata, and PSUs;
- harmonized definitions across survey waves.

## Hypothesis 4: the economically important problem is institutional linkage, not universal absence of records

### Claim to test

The same commercial event may be visible to a platform, merchant, payment
provider, statistical office, or tax authority under different identifiers and
rules, while no public evidence shows that these records are linked into one
measurement system.

### Proposed visibility matrix

Rows:

- merchant sale;
- marketplace transaction;
- messaging/social-commerce sale;
- payment;
- platform commission;
- delivery payout;
- merchant business record.

Columns:

- platform;
- merchant;
- payment provider;
- BPS;
- tax authority;
- public investor/researcher.

Each cell must be classified from evidence as:

- directly observed;
- survey-estimated;
- legally reportable;
- indirectly inferable;
- unknown.

### Evidence standard

A reporting law establishes a legal obligation, not actual implementation,
coverage, data quality, or successful linkage. A platform record establishes
private traceability, not government access. These must remain separate cells.

### Falsification or weakening condition

If institutional documentation establishes comprehensive, timely linkage of
the relevant records, then the fragmentation thesis weakens and the paper
should instead analyze how that integrated system measures digital activity.

### Required data

- regulations and implementation guidance;
- BPS questionnaires and processing documentation;
- platform reporting definitions;
- tax-authority evidence on reporting fields, coverage, timing, and use;
- explicit identification/linkage architecture where publicly documented.

## Hypothesis 5: measurement uncertainty is economically consequential, not merely clerical

### Claim to test

Definition, publication vintage, and business perimeter can alter substantive
comparisons of growth, platform ranking, or inferred commercial intensity.

### Existing support

ASEAN market estimates revise repeated country-year values, and issuer
transaction measures can also be revised. Global platform definitions produce
large cross-business differences in revenue-to-transaction ratios.

### Next analysis

For every headline comparison, separate:

1. source precision and rounding;
2. publication-vintage revision;
3. model dependence from country allocation;
4. definition dependence from revenue or transaction perimeter;
5. structural breaks from acquisitions, disposals, or principal-agent changes.

Then state which conclusions survive each source of uncertainty.

### Falsification or weakening condition

If reasonable source and definition choices do not change any substantive
comparison, measurement uncertainty remains an audit issue rather than a major
economic result.

## Proposed paper architecture

### Main evidence: Indonesia

1. Longitudinal issuer/segment histories admitted under explicit period,
   geography, and definition rules.
2. BPS official evidence on the broader e-commerce population and distribution.
3. Revenue-component and business-perimeter reconciliations.
4. Clearly conditional Grab and Shopee country constructions if retained.

### Supporting evidence: ASEAN

Six separate country histories establish recurrence, heterogeneity, and
publication-vintage sensitivity. Tax systems and platform responsibilities are
treated country by country. ASEAN observations are not pooled with Indonesia.

### Supporting evidence: global issuers

The eight-business panel demonstrates that transaction/revenue divergence and
definition dependence recur across different platform models. It is not a
representative global sample and does not validate Indonesia allocations.

### Optional evidence: investor response

Retain only if event timing, contamination, and market-price reproduction can be
closed. A large price file does not solve the economic identification problem.

## Candidate contribution statement

If the proposed tests survive, the paper could contribute:

> a source-auditable, multi-ledger account of Indonesia's digital-commerce
> transformation that shows how conclusions about scale, growth, participation,
> and visibility change across platform transaction measures, issuer revenue,
> merchant records, official estimates, and reporting institutions.

The novelty would not be the arithmetic identity between transaction value and
take rate. It would be the empirical reconciliation of the ledgers, the
measurement of consequential divergences, and the identification of the exact
links required before stronger GDP or tax conclusions become possible.

## Claims that remain prohibited without new evidence

- `transaction value - platform revenue = missing GDP`;
- `transaction value - platform revenue = tax gap`;
- businesses without financial statements are evading tax;
- platform records are available to or successfully linked by government;
- ASEAN countries share one institutional treatment;
- a purposive issuer panel identifies a population effect;
- more rows alone create causal identification.
