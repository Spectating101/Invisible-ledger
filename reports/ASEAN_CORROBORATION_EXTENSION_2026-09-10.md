# ASEAN corroboration extension for *Invisible Ledger*

## Technical summary

The ASEAN evidence supports a regional recurrence claim without treating
Southeast Asia as one homogeneous tax or platform market. Across Indonesia,
Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam, latest-vintage
e-Conomy SEA estimates show e-commerce GMV increasing between 2023 and 2025.
The median within-country increase is 36.4%, ranging from 12.5% in Singapore
to 53.8% in Malaysia.

The same evidence also validates Professor Kong's warning against pooling.
E-commerce represents very different shares of estimated digital-economy GMV
across countries (31.0% to 71.7% in 2025), platform-market structures differ,
and overlapping report vintages frequently revise earlier estimates. A second
2025 market source differs from e-Conomy SEA by -7.0% to +52.5% depending on
the country. These are definition and coverage diagnostics, not estimates of
which source is erroneous.

Accordingly, this module strengthens the paper in a limited but useful way:

1. Indonesia remains the proposed main issuer/accounting analysis.
2. ASEAN becomes six within-country corroborations of digital-commercial
   expansion and measurement sensitivity.
3. Tax systems and regulatory institutions remain country-specific evidence;
   no pooled ASEAN tax coefficient or common monetization assumption is used.
4. The regional evidence supports the claim that modern digital activity is
   large, dynamic, and measured through non-equivalent ledgers. It does not
   prove omitted GDP, hidden income, unpaid tax, or tax evasion.

## What was collected and constructed

Twenty-five official e-Conomy SEA PDFs were archived: regional reports from
2016–2022 and six country reports for each year from 2023–2025. The current
structured extraction uses the comparable country charts in the 2022–2025
vintages.

The source-vintage table contains 450 observations:

> 5 report vintages × 6 countries × 3 observation years per vintage × 5 GMV metrics.

The five metrics are overall digital-economy GMV, e-commerce GMV, transport
and food GMV, online-travel GMV, and online-media GMV. The canonical panel has
42 country-year rows covering every year from 2019 through 2025 for each
country. The 2020 observations come from the 2021 report's retrospectively
updated charts; no annual value was interpolated.

The reports and retrieval hashes are under
`sources/asean_economy_reports/`. The extraction and analysis are reproduced by
`scripts/analysis/build_asean_corroboration.py`.

## Digital commerce expands across all six countries, but at different rates

| Country | E-commerce GMV 2023 | E-commerce GMV 2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Indonesia | $59B | $71B | 20.3% |
| Malaysia | $13B | $20B | 53.8% |
| Philippines | $17B | $24B | 41.2% |
| Singapore | $8B | $9B | 12.5% |
| Thailand | $22B | $33B | 50.0% |
| Vietnam | $19B | $25B | 31.6% |

This is meaningful corroboration of a broad phenomenon: the expansion is not
unique to Indonesia and is not generated by aggregating countries first. Each
comparison is made within the same country using the latest publication
vintage available for both endpoints.

The result should not be interpreted as a common treatment effect. The six
countries differ in income, internet penetration, market structure, taxation,
regulation, and sector mix. The report therefore supplies external-validity
evidence for recurrence, not a pooled causal estimate.

## The digital-economy mix is not homogeneous

In 2025, e-commerce accounts for approximately 71.7% of estimated overall
digital-economy GMV in Indonesia, compared with 31.0% in Singapore. The other
countries fall between those values. This matters because an apparently
similar digital-economy total can represent very different combinations of
commerce, travel, media, and transport/food activity.

Momentum Works' 2025 platform estimates also show distinct country-market
structures. Shopee is the largest reported platform in each of the six rows,
but its reported share ranges from 49% to 58%, and the composition of the
remaining market differs: TikTok/Tokopedia and Blibli are particularly
relevant in Indonesia, while Lazada has different weights elsewhere and
Amazon appears in Singapore. The reported-platform HHI ranges from 3,726 to
4,894, subject to rounding and omitted sub-1% categories.

This evidence does not justify assuming that a platform's group monetization
rate, revenue definition, or compliance environment is constant across
countries. It provides a measurable reason not to do so.

## Revisions reveal measurement uncertainty, not economic change

The 450-row source table deliberately keeps all overlapping report vintages.
Of 60 repeated cells for overall digital-economy and e-commerce GMV, 45 change
between the earliest and latest publication. Among the changed cells, the
median absolute revision is 7.14%; the largest is Indonesia's 2019 e-commerce
estimate, revised from $21B in the 2021 report to $25B in the 2022 report
(19.05%). Vietnam's 2023 e-commerce estimate similarly moves from $16B in the
2023 report to $19B in the 2025 report (18.75%).

These changes are not year-to-year growth. They are successive estimates of
the same country-year. Silently combining vintages would therefore create
false movements. The canonical table applies one explicit rule—latest
available vintage—while the vintage table preserves the full audit trail.

This is directly relevant to *Invisible Ledger*: even reputable market
measurement systems revise the boundary and scale of digital activity as
their information improves. That is evidence of a measurement challenge, not
evidence that national accounts are wrong by the same amount.

## Different market sources do not define the same object

For 2025, the e-Conomy SEA e-commerce estimates are compared with Momentum
Works' platform-market estimates. The e-Conomy value is higher in five
countries and lower in Thailand. Relative differences range from -7.0% to
+52.5%.

The comparison is not used to average the sources or select the larger value.
Momentum Works notes that its GMV definition includes cancelled, returned,
refunded, and paid orders, while the e-Conomy reports apply their own market
model and scope. The differences therefore demonstrate definition and
coverage dependence. They are not error bounds and do not establish which
source is more accurate.

## Scale indicators retain the original ambition without making an invalid GDP claim

The canonical table joins the market estimates to World Bank GDP, household
consumption, population, and internet-use series at the country-year level.
For 2025, estimated e-commerce GMV ranges from about 1.49% to 5.72% of GDP
across the six countries. These ratios describe digital commercial intensity.

They do **not** mean that GDP is understated by that percentage. GMV is a gross
transaction flow, while GDP is value added; the same value can pass through
several transactions, and platform revenue, merchant revenue, consumption,
and value added are different accounting objects. The useful research point
is that the observable digital-transaction ledger has become economically
large relative to familiar national aggregates, while its mapping to issuer,
merchant, tax, and statistical ledgers remains incomplete and definition
dependent.

That is a stronger and more defensible continuation of the original idea than
adding ASEAN GMV to an Indonesia platform-revenue residual.

## Data-quality assessment

| Dimension | Assessment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Source authenticity | Strong | Official report PDFs are archived with URL, byte count, and SHA-256. |
| Extraction auditability | Strong with manual-transcription caveat | Every row names the report, country, vintage, metric, and PDF page. |
| Time coverage | Moderate | Six retained annual points per country; 2020 is absent and not fabricated. |
| Cross-vintage consistency | Measurably imperfect | Earlier values are revised; canonical selection and diagnostics are both retained. |
| Cross-country comparability | Moderate for market-scale description | Same report family and metric labels, but country institutions and sector structures differ. |
| Cross-source comparability | Weak for direct substitution | e-Conomy SEA and Momentum Works use different market definitions. |
| Tax inference | Not supported | Country tax systems are not pooled and GMV is not treated as a tax base. |
| Indonesia platform-revenue validation | Not provided | Regional GMV recurrence does not validate Grab/Shopee country-revenue allocations. |

## How this should enter the research design

The clean hierarchy is:

```text
Indonesia main analysis
  issuer/segment transaction and revenue records
  direct-versus-derived classification
  assumption and definition sensitivity

ASEAN corroboration
  six separate country histories
  digital-commercial scale and growth
  sector-mix and platform-structure heterogeneity
  publication-vintage and source-definition sensitivity

Country institutions
  tax and regulatory evidence evaluated country by country
  no pooled tax interpretation unless a separate harmonized design is built
```

This structure answers Kong's immediate concern. It expands time and regional
evidence without mixing samples in the main tables and without claiming that
ASEAN has a single tax system or platform business model.

## Limitations and next decisions

1. e-Conomy SEA values are market estimates credited to Bain analysis, not
   issuer-reported transactions or official national statistics.
2. The pre-2022 reports are archived but not yet admitted to the canonical
   panel because metric scope and chart structure changed; they require a
   separate comparability review.
3. The ASEAN module measures market scale, composition, and measurement
   sensitivity. It does not supply country platform revenue pairs.
4. Tax-policy and collection evidence should be added as country-specific
   institutional case material, not merged mechanically with GMV.
5. Advisor agreement is still required on the final main period and on the
   role of ASEAN in the paper.
