# Chapter 1. Introduction

Digital commerce has changed how goods and services are bought and sold. A seller in a small town can reach buyers across a country, a business can run from a mobile phone, and a customer can pay by scanning a code. Investors, statistics offices and tax authorities follow this activity through a handful of published figures.

Platform and online sales figures now carry very large sums. Businesses in 43 economies sold almost US$27 trillion online in 2022, by the United Nations' estimate (UNCTAD 2024), and platforms carry a growing part of that trade. Investors value platforms largely on their reported growth. If part of that growth is a platform keeping more of each sale, part of the share price rests on a pricing decision. Governments use the same figures to size their digital economies and to tax online sellers.

In Indonesia in 2023, two official figures for online commerce moved in opposite directions. The national statistics office, BPS, reported that online sales rose by about 41 percent (BPS 2025a). The central bank's measure of online shopping fell by about 5 percent (Bank Indonesia 2023; KabarBursa 2024).

Tokopedia, an Indonesian online marketplace, shows revenue and commerce moving apart inside one company. Between 2022 and 2023, its net revenue rose by 53 percent while its transaction value, the value of what was bought through the platform, fell by 9 percent (GoTo Gojek Tokopedia 2024a, 2024b).

Revenue and transaction value differ because a platform keeps only part of each sale, as commissions and fees. The rest is the seller's receipts, the driver's fare or another amount that passes through. This thesis calls what a revenue-only measure leaves out the invisible wedge. It does not mean activity that goes unrecorded.

The share a platform keeps is not fixed. When it rises, revenue can grow even as transaction value falls. At Tokopedia, about 60 percent of the extra revenue came from smaller discounts and incentives, which a platform pays out of its own earnings (GoTo Gojek Tokopedia 2024b).

Research explains why platform revenue and national sales figures can differ. It does not say when a figure stops moving with the activity it represents, or by how much. Answering that takes two things rarely found together. One is a platform's revenue and transaction value over several years. The other is a national count of online sellers whose underlying answers can be examined.

In Indonesia, several platforms report revenue and transaction value each year, and BPS asks online sellers when they began and how their sales changed. For 2024, BPS gives about US$81 billion for online sales and the central bank about US$31 billion, more than two to one (BPS 2025b; Bank Indonesia 2025).

The government has set new rules on platform commissions, discounts and tax. Its tax on marketplace sellers can raise about US$60 million a year on one measure of the market and about US$300 million on another, before exemptions (Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Indonesia 2025; author's calculation).

Most online sellers work through messaging and social media, outside the marketplaces, so platform and survey figures are likely to part. In 2023, traders at Tanah Abang, a wholesale market in Jakarta, said their customers had moved to sellers on TikTok Shop (Agence France-Presse 2023). The government then banned transactions on social media platforms, and TikTok Shop closed in Indonesia soon after (Ministry of Trade of the Republic of Indonesia 2023; Mandagie 2023). The events of 2023 suggest that the figures did part.
