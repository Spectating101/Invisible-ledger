# Abstract

**The Invisible Ledger: Quantifying the Invisible Wedge in Indonesia's Platform Economy**

Figures about the digital economy stand for activity that no one observes directly. A platform's revenue stands for the commerce it carries, and a survey's count of sellers stands for the businesses selling online. This thesis asks how much activity remains invisible when platforms are measured through their revenue, and when that choice changes the conclusion about growth. It studies Indonesia, using platform reports, BPS's survey of online sellers and national figures.

Revenue shows only a small part of the commerce. In 2023, three major platforms in Indonesia kept about US$3 billion of the US$43 billion that passed through them. Revenue also stopped tracking the commerce when platforms changed the share of each sale they kept. Indonesian and regional platforms changed that share about three times as much as 27 platforms abroad. Most of their large changes came between 2022 and 2024. So the hypothesis that revenue grows in proportion to the commerce is rejected.

The national count of online sellers rose by 27 percent in 2023. A test registered before the survey files were opened finds too few new sellers to produce that rise. A later, exploratory count shows that first-time entry explains only about half of it. More businesses account for most of the growth in online sales on the published figures. If the rest of the rise is read as wider survey coverage, that conclusion sits on its threshold.

The two results describe one problem. A figure stops tracking the activity it stands for when the link between them changes, through a platform's take rate or a survey's reach. The thesis also measures how far Indonesia's recent platform rules can reach: the new marketplace tax can collect from at most about 1 in 20 online sellers.

**Keywords:** digital platforms; take rate; e-commerce measurement; transaction value; online sellers; Indonesia
