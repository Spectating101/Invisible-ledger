# Chapter 5. Results

The results follow the two links of Figure 3.1. On the inner link, revenue showed a small and unsteady part of the commerce that platforms carried. Platforms changed how much of each sale they kept, and revenue moved with those choices (Sections 5.1 to 5.4). On the outer link, the national count of online sellers rose by 27 percent in 2023. About half of that rise did not come from sellers entering for the first time (Sections 5.5 to 5.10).

## 5.1 How much revenue leaves out

Revenue showed only a small part of what Indonesia's major platforms carried. In 2023, Tokopedia, Shopee and Grab together carried about US$43 billion of transaction value and kept about US$3 billion as revenue. Revenue was about 7 percent of that commerce, and the invisible wedge was about US$40 billion. The ecosystem ratio was about 13, so almost 13 dollars of commerce passed through the platforms for every dollar of revenue. Table 5.2 shows each platform.

**Table 5.2. The invisible wedge of three platforms, 2023 (US$ billion)**

| Platform | Transaction value | Revenue | Wedge | Ecosystem ratio | How it is labelled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tokopedia | 16.33 | 0.41 | 15.93 | 39.3 | Both direct segment disclosure |
| Shopee, Indonesia | 21.52 | 2.15 | 19.37 | 9.0 | Transaction value is an outside estimate (Momentum Works); revenue is derived at an assumed take rate of 10 percent |
| Grab, Indonesia | 5.38 | 0.60 | 4.78 | 7.9 | Revenue is a direct country disclosure; transaction value is derived from Grab's company-wide ratio |
| Total | 43.23 | 3.16 | 40.07 | 12.7 | Sum of three platforms, not a national total |

*Note:* Rounded amounts may not add. With Shopee's take rate at 9 or 11 percent, the total wedge is US$39.9 or US$40.3 billion; at Sea's company-wide rate of 11.5 percent, it is US$39.8 billion. Source: company filings; Momentum Works (2024); author's calculations.

The ratios differ because the amounts were obtained in different ways. Tokopedia reports both amounts, and its ratio is about 39. Shopee's ratio of 9 follows from the assumed take rate, and Grab's ratio of about 8 is its company-wide ratio. Neither is separate evidence for Indonesia. Blibli and Bukalapak also report both amounts, with ratios of about 43 and 36, close to Tokopedia's. They are left out of the sum because Blibli's figure includes online travel and Bukalapak reports for its whole group.

The size of the wedge does not depend on the assumed rates. A take rate for Shopee between 9 and 11 percent keeps the total near US$40 billion. Dropping any one platform still leaves more than US$20 billion. For platforms that report both amounts, the wedge was 97 to 99 percent of transaction value in every year. It moved almost one for one with the commerce. Appendix A gives the wedge by platform and year.

Platform commerce is itself only part of online commerce. In BPS's survey, marketplaces carry about a fifth of online sales value (Section 5.9), so platform revenue shows a smaller part still. A small share can still be a fair guide to the commerce, as long as it keeps its size from year to year.

## 5.2 Testing H1

Revenue did not keep pace with transaction value at the Indonesian and regional platforms, and the thesis rejects H1. At the typical platform in this group, the take rate moved by about 20 percent a year, up or down. At the typical foreign platform it moved by about 6 percent, so the moves were about three times as large. Table 5.3 gives the test and its checks.

**Table 5.3. The test of H1**

| Test | Method | Indonesian and regional group | Foreign group | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yearly change in log take rate, 5 platforms against 27 | Mann–Whitney | 0.20 | 0.06 | 0.004 |
| The same | Shuffle | 0.20 | 0.06 | 0.022 |
| Growth divergence in points, 5 platforms against 26 | Mann–Whitney | 27.0 | 6.5 | 0.005 |
| Drop each platform in turn, or treat Tokopedia and GoTo as one company | Mann–Whitney | 0.17 to 0.23 | 0.06 | 0.003 to 0.016 |
| Quarterly pairs, 3 platforms against 8 | Mann–Whitney | 0.071 | 0.049 | 0.19 |

*Note:* Group values are medians of each platform's median yearly change. Appendix B lists every check, including the tests without Ozon and Zalando.

The main test compares the five platforms with 27 abroad, one median per platform. Both methods of Section 4.3 reject equal moves, with p = 0.004 and p = 0.022. No single platform carries the result. Dropping any one, or treating Tokopedia and GoTo as one company, keeps the Mann–Whitney p-value at 0.016 or below. The main test was set up after a first, narrower test, so it is exploratory.

The test planned in advance was narrower: the three Indonesian marketplaces against the eight foreign platforms chosen first. It points the same way. With one median per platform, the Mann–Whitney test rejects equal moves (p = 0.024). The shuffle of platform labels comes close without passing (p = 0.06). Three platforms give a test little power. In that sample, revenue growth and transaction value growth had a correlation of 0.10 in Indonesia and 0.98 abroad, excluding Sea.

The direction of the moves is less telling than their size. Revenue grew faster than transaction value in 13 of the 15 Indonesian and regional pairs. In two of them, revenue and transaction value moved in opposite directions. Abroad, take rates also rose in about two thirds of pairs, but by much less. What sets the Indonesian and regional platforms apart is the size of the moves, which is what the test measures.

Quarterly pairs do not reject H1 (p = 0.19), contrary to a prediction registered before the test was run. The quarterly Indonesian pairs mostly cover 2023 to 2026, after most of the repricing, while the large moves came between 2021 and 2024. The rejection of H1 describes that period.

The thin share that Indonesian and regional platforms keep explains much of the threefold difference, an exploratory finding. Measured in percentage points, both groups changed their take rates by about 1 point a year. The difference between them is nowhere near large enough to count (p = 0.65).

The two groups differ in the share they start from. The typical Indonesian and regional platform keeps about 1.6 percent of each sale, against about 17 percent abroad. As Section 3.2 showed, the same 1-point rise then has very different effects. It lifts revenue by more than half at the thinner share and by about 6 percent at the thicker one. Figure 5.1 shows the three measures side by side. The test shows that take rates moved, but not what the platforms changed to move them.

## 5.3 What moved the take rate

Pricing, not more commerce, moved revenue at the Indonesian and regional platforms. Splitting each yearly move with equation (5), the take rate accounts for about two thirds of the move in revenue at the typical pair. Abroad it accounts for about a quarter. The wedge, by contrast, follows the commerce: in both groups, about 95 percent of a move in the wedge comes from transaction value.

Where companies disclose both their fees and their incentives, equation (6) splits the growth of revenue further. Table 5.4 gives the seven traced windows. Fees and incentives are measured per unit of transaction value, and a negative share means that a part pulled revenue down. The windows of one platform overlap, so they are not seven independent cases.

**Table 5.4. What moved revenue in seven Indonesian and regional windows (share of the growth of revenue, percent)**

| Platform and window | Growth of revenue | More commerce | Higher fees | Lower incentives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tokopedia, 2022–23 | +53% | −22 | 76 | 45 |
| Blibli third-party retail, 2022–23 | +465% | 17 | 20 | 63 |
| Blibli third-party retail, 2022–25 | +679% | 18 | 63 | 20 |
| Grab on-demand, 2021–24 | +321% | 34 | 34 | 33 |
| Grab on-demand, 2022–24 | +86% | 42 | 11 | 47 |
| GoTo on-demand, 2022–25 | +111% | 12 | 36 | 52 |
| GoTo on-demand, 2023–25 | +114% | 27 | 6 | 67 |

*Note:* Shares are rounded, so a row can add to 99 or 101. Source: company filings; author's calculations (Appendix A).

Higher fees and lower incentives together outweighed more commerce in every window. Lower incentives were the larger of the two in four windows and higher fees in two. In the seventh, Grab from 2021 to 2024, the two were nearly equal. Three series need a note. GoTo's 2022 transaction value is an estimate. Blibli does not report incentives, so its split rests on a proxy, and part of its 2022–23 rise comes from travel and digital products. Bukalapak's reports do not allow the parts to be separated.

Tokopedia can be read in two ways, and both point to pricing. Its incentives fell by about a third in 2023. Counted line by line in its accounts, lower incentives gave about 60 percent of the extra revenue. Higher gross fees gave about 40 percent. That count leaves the fall in volume inside both lines. Measured per unit of transaction value, as in Table 5.4, higher fees account for more of the rise in the take rate. The two views differ only on which lever led (Figure 5.2).

![Figure 5.2](figures/fig5_2_tokopedia_two_views.png)

**Figure 5.2. Tokopedia, 2022 to 2023: two ways to split the rise in revenue.** Left: the change in net revenue split by the lines of the accounts. Right: the growth of revenue split per unit of transaction value, where a negative share pulled revenue down. Source: GoTo annual report 2023, segment note; author's calculations.

Tokopedia is not the only case of revenue rising while the commerce shrank. From 2024 to 2025, Blibli's transaction value fell by about 2 percent while its revenue rose by about 12 percent. In the windows traced here, revenue growth was not a fair guide to the commerce the platforms carried. Whether markets treated it as one is a separate question.

## 5.4 What markets valued

Market value moved with transaction value, a registered result. The sample has 28 listed platforms and 138 platform-years. In it, a 1 percent rise in transaction value goes with a rise of about 0.6 percent in market value (p = 0.002). Section 4.5 describes this sample, which differs from the 27 foreign platforms of H1.

The second registered prediction does not hold in the main sample. It expected markets to value growth from a higher take rate less than growth from more commerce. A 1 percent rise in the take rate goes with a rise of about 0.3 percent, an estimate too uncertain to tell from zero. The gap between the two estimates is not large enough to count (p = 0.20). Trimming extreme values leaves it at p = 0.16.

A second run adds three segment series and three more whole companies. There the gap counts (p = 0.004), with about 0.6 for transaction value against about 0.1 for the take rate. That run sets segment figures against whole-company market values, as the registration warned, so the thesis does not rely on it. The answer depends on which platforms are in the sample.

Market value follows the commerce a platform carries. Early studies found a similar link, with markets pricing measures of web activity beyond revenue (Trueman, Wong and Zhang 2000). The data do not show that markets valued growth from pricing any less. Revenue, transaction value and market value all come from the platforms' own reports. The national figures give a separate view of the same commerce.

## 5.5 The platforms and the sellers in 2023

Buying through platforms did not boom in 2023. Household spending rose by about 9 percent in current prices that year. Of six measures of buying through platforms, five grew more slowly, and three fell. Blibli is the one exception. Figure 5.3 sets the measures side by side, each in its own unit.

The figures that described a boom were of other kinds. Tokopedia's revenue rose by 53 percent and BPS's online sales value by 41 percent, while the buying that platforms carried grew slowly or fell. Figure 5.3 shows the two kinds in different colours.

![Figure 5.3](figures/fig5_3_growth_2023.png)

**Figure 5.3. Growth in 2023 (percent).** Blue bars show measures of buying; orange bars show the figures that described a boom. Each measure is in its own unit. Sources: company reports, Bank Indonesia, Momentum Works and BPS; household spending from national accounts. GoTo's change rests on an estimated 2022 value, and Momentum Works' 3.5 is the author's calculation from its published levels (its 2024 release states 3.7). Appendix D gives the values.

Sellers' own accounts of their sales show no boom either, a registered result. BPS asks each business how its online sales changed. For businesses already selling, the registered prediction set an upper limit on the median change. The limit was about 10 percent, the published growth in sales per business. The median change was zero.

Fewer sellers reported a rise than a year earlier. About a quarter reported a rise and about a third a fall. A year before, more than a third had reported a rise and a quarter a fall. The mean change was about −4 percent. Lack of demand became the main obstacle, named by 41 percent of sellers against 35 percent in 2022. The published count of online sellers nonetheless rose by 27 percent in 2023. A rise of that size, in a year without a boom in sales, needs explaining.

## 5.6 H2 and the entry test

On BPS's published figures, H2 holds. From 2022 to 2023, online sales value grew by 41 percent and the number of businesses by 27 percent. More businesses account for 71 percent of that growth, and higher sales per business for the rest.

H2 holds again from 2023 to 2024. Value grew by 17 percent, and more businesses account for 90 percent of the growth, or 71 percent on BPS's other count for 2023. The split of equation (7) is arithmetic, and it cannot tell a new seller from one counted for the first time.

The registered entry test separates new sellers from sellers counted for the first time, and it fails. In the survey for 2023 (the 2024 file), about 13 percent of sellers began selling online that year. The test required 21.5 percent. Even with no sellers stopping, that many new sellers would raise the count by at most about 16 percent. The published rise was 27 percent.

Recall error does not explain the shortfall. As Section 3.5 noted, sellers who report a start date later than the true one make new sellers look more numerous. Such errors push the share toward passing, so the shortfall is, if anything, understated. Part of the 2023 rise in the count is something other than first-time entry, and its size is the next result.

## 5.7 What the rise in the count contains

About half of the 2023 rise in the count is not explained by first-time entry, an exploratory result. Table 5.5 sets out the accounting. In the survey for 2023, sellers who began before 2023 outnumber the whole count for 2022 by about 306 thousand. They make up 37 percent of the rise if no seller stopped, and 56 percent if sellers stopped at about 5 percent a year.

**Table 5.5. The 2023 rise in the count of online sellers (thousands)**

| Part of the rise | If no seller stopped | If sellers stopped at 5 percent a year |
|---|---|---|
| Rise between the weighted totals of the two files | 819 (100%) | 819 (100%) |
| Net entry: sellers who began in 2023, less those who stopped | 513 (63%) | 361 (44%) |
| Earlier sellers newly counted above the whole 2022 count | 306 (37%) | 458 (56%) |

*Note:* BPS's published counts give a rise of 821 thousand (BPS 2025). Source: BPS survey files, weighted; author's calculations.

The cohort counts show the break directly. A cohort should only shrink between surveys, and the cohorts did so in every interval except 2023. Sellers who began by 2020 fell by about 10 percent between the surveys for 2020 and 2022. In the survey for 2023 they rose by 6 percent.

The count behaved normally again in BPS's published table for 2024. The cohort that began before 2020 shrank by about 10 percent. At least 754 thousand new sellers appeared, more than the rise of 584 thousand.

The break was uneven across places and channels. Across the 34 provinces, growth in the count had almost no link to the share of new sellers, a rank correlation of 0.10. It had only a weak link to growth in the sample, at 0.35. A rank correlation compares the order of provinces on two measures and runs from −1 to 1. The break sat in chat and social selling. Among sellers who began by 2022, sellers using only chat or social media rose by 13 percent, and marketplace users by 0.3 percent.

Sampling noise does not explain the break. BPS's published error rates put the usual size of the sampling noise in the national count at about 1.4 percent. The 306 thousand extra sellers are about 4.6 times that noise in the change between the two years. The calculation applies the 2024 edition's error rates to both years and treats the two surveys as independent.

Most of the 2023 rise in value has no source in the answers of existing sellers. BPS's online sales value rose from about US$53 billion to about US$72 billion. Entrants account for 25 to 30 percent of that rise, and sellers not explained by entry for 15 to 17 percent. Existing sellers reported a mean change of about −4 percent, so the remaining 53 to 59 percent cannot be traced to them.

The checks point to a survey that reached more chat and social sellers in 2023, the kind of break that Section 3.5 described. The files cannot separate that reading from sellers returning after a pause. Who the extra sellers were bears on the choice between the two.

## 5.8 Who the extra sellers were

The extra count leaned toward long-running businesses, older owners and places outside Java, an exploratory result. Each group's extra count is its 2023 count above what its own yearly change from 2020 to 2022 would give (Section 4.7). The extra sellers total about 450 thousand, close to the 458 thousand in Table 5.5. Figure 5.4 compares each group's share of the extra count with its share of the 2022 count.

![Figure 5.4](figures/fig5_4_extra_sellers.png)

**Figure 5.4. Who the extra sellers were.** The extra sellers are the 2023 count above what each group's normal yearly change in 2020 to 2022 would give, about 450 thousand in total. Source: BPS survey files, weighted; author's calculations. Exploratory.

The extra count did not lean toward young businesses. A lean toward men in the raw counts disappears once each group's normal change is taken out. This profile bears on whether the extra sellers were returning after a pause.

Part-year selling weighs against returners. A returning seller would usually sell for only part of the year, unless it restarted in January, yet part-year selling fell in 2023. Among sellers who began by 2021, those selling for part of the year fell from about 470 thousand to about 320 thousand. The same fall appears for the groups that began by 2020 and by 2022.

The age profile weighs against returners as well. Small firms that stop are more often young firms with young owners (McKenzie and Paffhausen 2019). Returners would then make the extra count lean young. It leaned old.

Returning sellers still cannot be ruled out. Returners equal to 10 to 15 percent of the 2022 count would remove the unexplained part, which is a scenario and not an estimate. The files carry no linked histories, and BPS changed how it asks about months between the rounds. Exits, or a move to full-year selling, could also offset part-year returners. Where online sellers sell, and what records they keep, complete the survey evidence.

## 5.9 Channels and records

Most online selling happens outside marketplaces, and records are thinner there. The path of marketplace use, the value held by sellers without financial statements and the World Bank results were registered. The comparison within the group that began before 2020 is exploratory.

Nearly all online sellers, about 96 percent, use chat or social media. Marketplace use fell every time it was measured, from about 22 percent of sellers in 2020 to about 17 percent in 2024. BPS worded the question slightly differently for 2023 and 2024.

Almost all of the growth in value came from outside the marketplaces. From 2023 to 2024, BPS's marketplace value grew by about 1.5 percent and other channels by about 21 percent. BPS's marketplace category is itself broad: 43 percent of its sellers use only Gojek or Grab, and 47 percent use a shopping marketplace.

![Figure 5.5](figures/fig5_5_marketplace_and_records.png)

**Figure 5.5. Marketplace use and financial statements, 2020 to 2024.** Marketplace use for 2020, 2022 and 2023 comes from the survey files and for 2024 from BPS's published figure. Financial statements come from the survey files. BPS worded the marketplace question slightly differently for 2023 and 2024, and the survey has no point for 2021. Source: BPS; author's calculations.

Records are thinner outside the marketplaces. In 2023, about 29 percent of marketplace sellers kept complete financial statements, against about 12 percent of other sellers. Figure 5.5 shows the share of all sellers who keep them falling. The fall from 2020 to 2022 came while the survey appears to have counted the same kinds of seller. The fall in 2023 may partly reflect the chat-only sellers added to the count.

The fall reaches long-running sellers too, an exploratory finding. Among sellers who began before 2020, the share keeping complete statements fell from about 23 percent in 2022 to about 17 percent in 2023.

Sellers without statements hold far less of the value than of the count. On the bracket scenarios of Section 4.8, they held 26 to 40 percent of online value in 2022. They made up 79 percent of the count. The annual reading of the brackets passes the validation rule, at 1.95 times BPS's total. The monthly reading fails, at 23 times. The lower brackets alone exceed BPS's total, so the shares are ranges.

One registered prediction on value failed narrowly. It expected the value share of sellers without statements to fall by no more than 5 points between 2020 and 2022. The share fell by 5.8 points on the main version and by 3.3 points on a second version.

World Bank surveys of Indonesian firms show the same thin records. In six Indonesian cities, 27 percent of unregistered firms use social media with their customers. Among those, 70 percent keep no written records, and their median sales are about US$4,000 a year. A registered prediction that their mean sales would stay below about US$6,600 failed, at about US$7,300.

Among formal firms, about 40 percent in Indonesia have neither a website nor online tax filing. That is the highest share of seven countries, against about 9 percent in Malaysia and in Thailand.

The split by channel bears on the third question of Section 2.4, because a rule built on platform records reaches only the marketplace part. Chapter 6 measures that reach. The same split also marks where the national figures part.

## 5.10 Why the national figures disagree

The national figures agree on growth only where they cover the same sellers, and they do not agree on scale. In 2023, Bank Indonesia's e-commerce figure fell by about 5 percent while BPS's rose by about 41 percent. That is the disagreement with which Chapter 1 began, and the third objective asks what lies behind it.

Where Bank Indonesia's figure and BPS's cover the same ground, the growth is small. From 2022 to 2023, BPS's own marketplace slice grew by about 0.2 percent in the middle scenario. Across the scenarios it grew by 0.2 to 13 percent. From 2023 to 2024 it grew by about 1.5 percent. Most of the disagreement sits outside the marketplaces, among the sellers that only BPS reaches.

A registered test on size gives a mixed answer. It predicted that the part of BPS's marketplace sales that went to final consumers in 2022 would fall below Bank Indonesia's figure. The prediction holds in the low and middle scenarios, at 0.16 and 0.68 times Bank Indonesia's figure. It fails in the high one, at 1.62 times.

In 2024 the figures divide by coverage. Those that cover mainly platforms grew by 5 to 7 percent, and BPS's grew by about 17 percent. The adjacent figures of Table 4.3 point the same way as BPS but do not measure Indonesian e-commerce. Parcels shipped in Southeast Asia grew by about 25 percent, and the tax on foreign digital services by about 25 percent.

Payment figures grew faster than any of these from 2023 to 2024. E-money shopping grew by about 30 percent, mobile-banking purchases by 83 percent and QRIS payments by 187 percent. They are context only, because they include in-person payments and the switch away from cash.

Levels cannot be reconciled. In 2024, BPS put online sales at about US$81 billion and Bank Indonesia at about US$31 billion. In 2023, BPS's marketplace slice was about US$13 billion, against about US$38 billion for Tokopedia and the Shopee estimate together. In 2024 it was less than a quarter of Momentum Works' marketplace estimate of about US$57 billion.

Platform figures and the seller survey count different things. Platform transaction value includes shipping, digital goods, travel and offline sales that a seller survey does not count in the same way. The counts of sellers differ too. Tokopedia alone said that it had about 15 million merchants in 2022, while BPS counts about 0.6 million marketplace sellers.

Platform sales do not always keep pace with household spending. In each of the four quarters to mid-2026, GoTo's on-demand transaction value grew more slowly than household spending. In the last, it grew by 2 percent against 8 percent, while its revenue grew by 21 percent. Grab's and Shopee's figures grew faster, but they cover the region.

The results answer the research question at two levels. On the inner link, revenue showed a small part of the commerce and stopped being a fair guide when the take rate moved. On the outer link, the 2023 rise in the count of sellers was only partly first-time entry. The figures part most where only the survey looks. Chapter 6 turns to the rules that Indonesia is writing.
