## 5. Where her requests collide with something we have

1. **Settled decisions she overrides.** The 1.1 ambition paragraph (she rewrote it), the "abbreviations not added" rule, the Symbols page, the "kept as it is" status of the registered/hypotheses tables. Recorded in `DECISIONS.md`.
2. **Dropping 3.3 while Chapters 5 and 6 depend on it.** Equation (5) (volume effect plus take-rate effect) and equation (6) (m = f − s) are what Table 11, Figure 3, and Section 6.2 (the commission cap acts on f, the discount rule acts on s) use. She also drops Table 11. Possible reading: the decomposition is not a hypothesis test, so it leaves the main text; the identity m = f − s survives as one sentence in 3.2 so the policy chapter still works. *Question.*
3. **Replacing Δln with simple percentage change touches more than one equation.**
   - Eq. (5) is exact in logs (Δln R = Δln V + Δln m). With simple changes, g(R) = g(V) + g(m) + g(V)g(m): an extra product term, so the "volume share" and "take-rate share" of a movement no longer add to 100 percent without a stated convention.
   - Eq. (8) reads as an elasticity today ("a 1 percent rise in transaction value went with about 0.6 percent more market value", Section 5.4 and Chapter 7). With simple changes it becomes a semi-elasticity and the sentence changes.
   - The headline for H1, "the take rate moved about three times as much" (0.20 against 0.06), is a median **log** change. In simple percentage changes the numbers and p-values differ. Needs recomputing before the abstract, 1.1, 5.2 and Chapter 7 can be touched.
   - Everything recomputed is **exploratory** or registered under its original definition; the label must say which.
4. **Registered / exploratory / descriptive.** All the new regressions are post-registration, so they are exploratory. The mandatory registered tests keep their original outcome and wording; Appendix C stays.
5. **Licensed data.** Whatever needs BPS microdata or LSEG cannot be rerun here. The empirical package cannot ship those inputs.
6. **Table 3.2 holds the registered "what would count against it" column.** She drops it for the slides. The prose must still say, once and plainly, what result would have counted against each hypothesis, or the test is no longer falsifiable on the page.
