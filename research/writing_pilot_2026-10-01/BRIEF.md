# Writing pilot brief: Introduction of the Invisible Ledger thesis

Write Chapter 1 (Introduction) of a finance master's thesis, 650-850 words, in English. Output plain markdown: a "# 1. Introduction" heading, paragraphs, then "## 1.1 Research question and hypotheses". No bullet lists in the body except the hypothesis list.

## Writing rules (hard)
Every sentence must translate cleanly into junior-high-level Chinese AND junior-high-level Vietnamese. Do not write translations; use the two languages only as a filter. In practice:
1. Common words only: understand, show, use, enough, same, change, grow, fall, keep. Never comprehend, elucidate, adequacy, salient, nuanced, leverage (verb), underscore, furthermore, moreover, notably.
2. At most two nouns stacked together ("platform revenue" is fine; "platform revenue growth divergence measure" is not).
3. Point first: subject, then what happened, then why.
4. Active voice. First person "I" for the author's choices.
5. No idioms or phrasal-verb shortcuts.
6. One idea per sentence. If a sentence needs "while", "whereas" or "rather than" to hold two ideas, split it.
7. No hedging inside the story: no "can", "may", "might", "arguably", "it is possible that". The data below are facts; state them plainly. Do NOT add caveats or limitations in the introduction except ONE short closing sentence pointing to Chapter 7 (limitations).
8. Never use "Overall," "therefore", "Against this backdrop", "not X but Y" or "rather than".
9. Technical words allowed (define each once, in plain words, the first time): transaction value, revenue, take rate, GDP, BPS (Indonesia's statistics office), the wedge (W), the growth gap (D). Avoid the word "indicator" and the word "proxy".
10. Each number appears once in the chapter.

## The chain (follow this order, one or two paragraphs per step)
1. Two numbers describe a platform: transaction value (everything sold through it) and revenue (what the platform keeps). People use revenue to judge business and the size of the digital economy. Picture: a night-market owner takes a cut from every stall (use this image once, briefly).
2. They can split. Tokopedia, 2022 to 2023: transaction value fell 8.9 percent; revenue rose 53.2 percent. Of the revenue increase, 60.6 percent came from giving smaller customer discounts (incentives) and 39.4 percent from higher gross revenue (fees). Investors reading revenue saw growth; anyone reading transaction value saw decline.
3. It happens repeatedly. At the three Indonesian platforms that report both numbers (Tokopedia, Blibli, Bukalapak), there are eight year-to-year comparisons (2020-2025). Revenue grew faster in six. The two moved in opposite directions twice. The typical (median) gap was 38.92 percentage points. At eight large listed platforms outside Indonesia (eBay, Etsy, Jumia, Mercado Libre, Rakuten, Sea, Shopify, Zalando; 29 comparisons) the typical gap was 7.1 points.
4. It happens nationally. BPS: from 2023 to 2024, total e-commerce sales grew 17.08 percent; sales through marketplaces grew 1.45 percent; other channels grew 20.57 percent. So 98.46 percent of the new growth happened outside marketplaces. In 2023, 95.33 percent of e-commerce businesses sold through instant messaging. Growth came mostly from more businesses selling online (+15.31 percent) and little from each business selling more (+1.54 percent).
5. The wedge. W = transaction value minus revenue. It is the money the platform does not keep: what sellers and drivers receive, plus the discounts buyers get. In 2023, Tokopedia, Shopee and Grab together handled US$43.23 billion of transaction value and kept US$3.16 billion as revenue; the wedge was US$40.07 billion. The growth gap D = revenue growth minus transaction-value growth; it shows when the platform's cut changes.
6. Why it matters: revenue is the number investors watch and the one easiest to mistake for the size of the economy. It is a safe guide only while the platform's cut stays steady.
7. Setting: Indonesia, the largest digital economy in Southeast Asia (it grew from about US$100 billion in 2020 to US$263 billion in 2024 for the region, Google, Temasek and Bain). Shopee and Grab are also studied but their Indonesian numbers must be built from company-wide figures, so they are used only for the 2023 size comparison.

## Research question (keep this meaning; plain words)
When can a platform's revenue stand in for the sales it carries, and when does using revenue instead change what we conclude about size, growth or direction?

## Hypotheses (keep the four; plain words; one sentence each)
H1: Within the same platform, transaction value and revenue do not grow at the same rate.
H2: Indonesia's e-commerce growth comes mainly from more businesses selling online, not from each business selling more.
H3: Businesses that sell on marketplaces are more likely to keep full financial records (28.63 percent vs 12.25 percent keep complete financial statements).
H4: What we conclude about the growth of Indonesian digital commerce changes with the number we use to measure it.

(H2's "not from" wording is allowed inside the hypothesis only.)

Close with one sentence: the limits of this evidence are set out in Chapter 7.
