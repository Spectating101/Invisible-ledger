# 1. Introduction

Two numbers describe an online platform. The first is transaction value, which is the total money spent on everything sold through the platform. The second is revenue, which is the money the platform keeps. Picture a night-market owner who takes a small cut from every stall. The sales of all the stalls are the transaction value. The cut is the revenue.

People use revenue to judge a business. They also use it to judge the size of the digital economy. This is easy to do, because revenue is the number that companies report most clearly.

The two numbers split apart. Tokopedia shows this from 2022 to 2023. Its transaction value fell 8.9 percent. Its revenue rose 53.2 percent. Of the revenue increase, 60.6 percent came from smaller customer discounts, which are called incentives. The other 39.4 percent came from higher gross revenue, which means more fees. An investor who read revenue saw growth. A person who read transaction value saw decline.

This happens again and again. Three Indonesian platforms report both numbers: Tokopedia, Blibli and Bukalapak. They give eight year-to-year comparisons from 2020 to 2025. Revenue grew faster in six of them. The two numbers moved in opposite directions twice. The typical gap, which is the median, was 38.92 percentage points. I also looked at large listed platforms outside Indonesia: eBay, Etsy, Jumia, Mercado Libre, Rakuten, Sea, Shopify and Zalando. They give 29 comparisons. The typical gap there was 7.1 points.

The split shows up in national data too. BPS is Indonesia's statistics office. From 2023 to 2024, BPS reports that total e-commerce sales grew 17.08 percent. Sales through marketplaces grew 1.45 percent. Sales through other channels grew 20.57 percent. So 98.46 percent of the new growth happened outside marketplaces. Instant messaging is a main channel for these sales. In 2023, 95.33 percent of e-commerce businesses sold through it. Growth came mostly from more businesses selling online, up 15.31 percent. Each business sold only a little more, up 1.54 percent.

I use two simple measures to follow the split. The first is the wedge, which I write as W. W is transaction value minus revenue. It is the money the platform does not keep. Sellers and drivers receive part of it. Buyers receive the rest as discounts. In 2023, Tokopedia, Shopee and Grab together handled US$43.23 billion of transaction value. They kept US$3.16 billion as revenue. The wedge was US$40.07 billion. The second measure is the growth gap, which I write as D. D is revenue growth minus transaction-value growth. D shows when the platform's cut changes.

This matters because revenue is the number investors watch. It is also the number easiest to mistake for the size of the economy. Revenue is a safe guide only while the platform's cut stays steady. When the cut changes, revenue tells a different story from the sales behind it.

I study Indonesia. It is the largest digital economy in Southeast Asia. The digital economy of the whole region grew from about US$100 billion in 2020 to US$263 billion in 2024, according to Google, Temasek and Bain. Tokopedia, Blibli and Bukalapak give me the main platform data. I also study Shopee and Grab. Their Indonesian numbers must be built from company-wide figures. For this reason I use them only for the size comparison in 2023.

## 1.1 Research question and hypotheses

My research question is simple. When does a platform's revenue stand in for the sales it carries? And when does using revenue instead of those sales change what we conclude about size, growth or direction?

I test four hypotheses.

- H1: Within the same platform, transaction value and revenue do not grow at the same rate.
- H2: Indonesia's e-commerce growth comes mainly from more businesses selling online, not from each business selling more.
- H3: Businesses that sell on marketplaces are more likely to keep full financial records. Of these businesses, 28.63 percent keep complete financial statements, against 12.25 percent for the others.
- H4: What we conclude about the growth of Indonesian digital commerce changes with the number we use to measure it.

The limits of this evidence are set out in Chapter 7.
