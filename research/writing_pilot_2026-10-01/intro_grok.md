# 1. Introduction

Two numbers describe a platform. Transaction value is everything sold through the platform. Revenue is what the platform keeps. People use revenue to judge a business. People also use revenue to judge the size of the digital economy. A night-market owner takes a cut from every stall. The cut is the owner's revenue. The stall sales are the transaction value. The platform works in the same way.

The two numbers do not stay together. At Tokopedia, from 2022 to 2023, transaction value fell 8.9 percent. Revenue rose 53.2 percent. Of the revenue increase, 60.6 percent came from giving smaller customer discounts. These discounts are incentives. The other 39.4 percent came from higher gross revenue. Gross revenue is fees. Investors who read revenue saw growth. Anyone who read transaction value saw decline. A reader who sees only revenue thinks the platform grew. A reader who sees only transaction value thinks the platform shrank.

This happens many times. Three Indonesian platforms report both numbers. They are Tokopedia, Blibli, and Bukalapak. I count eight year-to-year comparisons from 2020 to 2025. Revenue grew faster in six comparisons. The two numbers moved in opposite directions in two comparisons. The typical gap is the median gap. The typical gap was 38.92 percentage points. Eight large listed platforms outside Indonesia report both numbers. They are eBay, Etsy, Jumia, Mercado Libre, Rakuten, Sea, Shopify, and Zalando. I count 29 comparisons. The typical gap was 7.1 points. The Indonesian gap is wider.

The same thing appears in national data. BPS is Indonesia's statistics office. From 2023 to 2024, total e-commerce sales grew 17.08 percent. Sales through marketplaces grew 1.45 percent. Sales through other channels grew 20.57 percent. Of the new growth, 98.46 percent happened outside marketplaces. Marketplace sales grew very little. Other channels produced almost all of the new sales. In 2023, 95.33 percent of e-commerce businesses sold through instant messaging. Growth came mostly from more businesses selling online. The number of businesses selling online rose 15.31 percent. Each business sold only a little more. Sales per business rose 1.54 percent.

I name one number the wedge. The wedge W equals transaction value minus revenue. W is the money the platform does not keep. W is what sellers and drivers receive, plus the discounts buyers get. In 2023, Tokopedia, Shopee, and Grab together had US$43.23 billion of transaction value. They kept US$3.16 billion as revenue. The wedge was US$40.07 billion. Most of the money on these platforms is not revenue. I also name a second number the growth gap. The growth gap D equals revenue growth minus transaction-value growth. D shows when the platform's cut changes. I call this cut the take rate. The take rate is the share of transaction value that the platform keeps as revenue.

Revenue is the number investors watch. Revenue is also the number that people treat as GDP. GDP is the total value of goods and services a country produces. Revenue is a safe guide only if the take rate stays steady. A steady take rate keeps revenue growth close to transaction-value growth. A changing take rate makes D large.

I study Indonesia. Indonesia is the largest digital economy in Southeast Asia. The region's digital economy grew from about US$100 billion in 2020 to US$263 billion in 2024. Google, Temasek, and Bain report these numbers. I also study Shopee and Grab. I must build their Indonesian numbers from company-wide figures. I use Shopee and Grab only for the 2023 size comparison. I use Tokopedia, Blibli, and Bukalapak for the year-to-year tests.

## 1.1 Research question and hypotheses

I ask one question. When does a platform's revenue match the sales it carries? When does using revenue instead change what we conclude about size, growth, or direction?

I test four hypotheses.

- H1: Within the same platform, transaction value and revenue do not grow at the same rate.
- H2: Indonesia's e-commerce growth comes mainly from more businesses selling online, not from each business selling more.
- H3: Businesses that sell on marketplaces are more likely to keep full financial records (28.63 percent vs 12.25 percent keep complete financial statements).
- H4: What we conclude about the growth of Indonesian digital commerce changes with the number we use to measure it.

The limits of this evidence are set out in Chapter 7.
