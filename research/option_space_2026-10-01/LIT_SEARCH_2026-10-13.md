# Literature search for Chapter 2 (13 Oct 2026)

Purpose: test gap question 1 (has anyone measured platform take rates over time?), find finance sources on how investors read platform figures, and check the findings Chapter 2 attributes to cited studies. Web searches only; abstracts and publisher or repository pages, not full texts. Treat every line below as "to confirm against the paper" before final submission.

## A. Studies of platform commissions (bear on gap question 1)

| Study | What it does | Bearing on the thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Li and Wang, "Regulating Powerful Platforms: Evidence from Commission Fee Caps", Information Systems Research 36(1), 126-140 (online 2024, DOI 10.1287/isre.2022.0191) | Commission caps on food delivery in 14 US cities and states; independent restaurants lost orders and revenue, chains gained, platforms raised consumer delivery fees | Commission changes studied for their effects on participants, not as a measurement question |
| Han et al. (TSE conference paper, 2024), creator platform commission rise from 5 to 20 percent in 2019 | Difference-in-differences on 7,186 creators over 51 months | Same: effect on participants; unpublished |
| Yu, "Commission Fee Structure and Innovation in Digital Platforms" (AEA 2025 programme) | Commission structure and innovation | Same; working paper |
| Binns and Stein (University of Oxford with Worker Info Exchange, 2025) | UK Uber driver data, 2016-2024, 1.5 million trips: median take rate rose from 25 to 29 percent after dynamic pricing (2023) | A take rate tracked over time, from drivers' data for one platform; advocacy-linked; company disputes it |
| COSTING journal (2026), Shopee and Tokopedia administrative fee increases | Qualitative, Islamic business ethics | Documents fee rises in Indonesia; no measurement |

Reading: take rates are studied, but as prices whose changes affect sellers, drivers or restaurants, measured from one platform's fee schedule or users' data. None found asks whether a platform's reported revenue remains a fair guide to the commerce it carries, uses reported revenue against reported transaction value, or covers Indonesian platforms. Gap question 1 should be narrowed to that claim; "no study follows the share over several years" is too broad.

## B. How investors and analysts read platform figures (finance section)

| Study | Finding (from abstract) |
|---|---|
| Givoly, Li, Lourie and Nekrasov (2019), Review of Accounting Studies 24(4) | KPI news carries information beyond earnings for many KPIs; less when the computation is not disclosed or changes; analysts forecast KPIs more when earnings are less informative; KPI forecasts beat mechanical ones |
| Tang (2025), Journal of Business Finance and Accounting | Analysts' KPI questions on calls lead managers to disclose more KPIs; stronger when earnings are less relevant |
| Trueman, Wong and Zhang (2000), JAR supplement; (2001), RAST | Web traffic relates to internet stock prices beyond financial statements; helps forecast revenue |
| Rajgopal, Kotha and Venkatachalam (2000 working paper; 2003 JAR) | Web traffic value relevant; about US$700 per unique monthly visitor in 1999 (working paper), marked down in 2000 |
| McCarthy, Fader and Hardie (2017), Journal of Marketing 81(1), 17-35 | Customer-based corporate valuation from publicly disclosed customer data |
| U.S. SEC (2020), Release 33-10751 | Interpretive guidance: a key metric needs a clear definition and method, why it is useful, and discussion of changes in method and their effects |
| Liu, Nissim and Thomas (2002), JAR 40(1) | Valuation by multiples (reserve; finding to be confirmed from the paper before use) |
