Known risks from AGENTS.md, resolved:
- **Kleven et al. (2011)** is real (Econometrica 79(3), 651-692, DOI 10.3982/ECTA9113) and the Chapter 2 sentence fits its main finding. One narrowing: the paper's contrast is third-party-reported income (employers, but also banks and other third parties) against self-reported income, not employers only. The Chapter 2 sentence gives no effect sizes, so no size claim needs checking. The working-paper version gives 0.3 percent evasion on third-party-reported income and 37 percent on self-reported income; if numbers are ever added, take them from the Econometrica text, not from the working paper.
- **Liu, Nissim and Thomas** is not cited anywhere in the thesis folder. It appears only in REFERENCES_WORKING.md (section B, reserve). Drop it from the list unless a finance section is added.
- **Li and Wang** is Information Systems Research 36(1), 126-140, March 2025, DOI 10.1287/isre.2022.0191. The abstract supports every part of the ch6:106 sentence (14 US cities and states; independent restaurants lost orders and revenue; chains gained; platforms raised consumer delivery fees). Only the abstract was read.
- **Trueman, Wong and Zhang: 2000 or 2001?** Both papers exist, and both should stay if both are cited. The 2000 paper ("The eyeballs have it", Journal of Accounting Research 38, Supplement, 137-162) is about stock prices. The 2001 paper ("Back to basics", Review of Accounting Studies 6(2-3), 305-329) is about forecasting revenue. The proposal's single entry (2001) matched the proposal's use, "web activity and revenue forecasts". The thesis sentences in ch3:179 and ch5:118 are about how markets priced web activity, so **2000 is the right paper for them**. Their wording needs one fix: the 2000 paper found web-traffic measures added explanatory power beyond *earnings and other financial data* (net income was not priced, while gross profit was). It does not test web traffic "beyond revenue". In ch2:13, citing both is fine only if the sentence covers valuation and revenue forecasting. As written ("how investors valued those firms"), 2000 and Rajgopal et al. (2003) are enough.

Top problems, ranked:
1. **Bank Indonesia (2025) is cited for a figure Bank Indonesia never published in a 2025 document** (ch1:5, ch1:15, ch2:17, ch4:127). The 2023 fall of about 5 percent compares Rp476.3 trillion (2022) with Rp453.75 trillion (2023). The 2023 value is known only from a news report of a BI press conference (KabarBursa, 18 Jan 2024). The repo labels it "news" (`tables/src/digital_rulers_annual.csv`), and `bps_bridge_NOTES.txt` item 3 says BI's own texts do not print the 2022 or 2023 outturn. The 2024 value (about Rp487 trillion, about US$31 billion) comes from a Cerita BI page of 2025. That page reports "sekitar Rp 487 triliun pada 2024", the same number BI had forecast for 2024 in its 2023 annual meeting release. The proposal's entry ("Payment System Statistics (SPIP) and QRIS reports") contains none of these figures. **Fix:** cite the three documents separately (see the list in section 3). Say once, in Chapter 7, that the 2023 value rests on a press report of a BI statement. The ch1:5 sentence ("The two official figures moved in opposite directions") is the thesis's opening hook, so this matters most.
2. **GoTo Gojek Tokopedia (2024), the press release, does not show the 53 percent revenue rise cited in ch1:7.** The 19 March 2024 release shows e-commerce GTV down 9 percent (Rp273,146 billion to Rp248,836 billion: matches) but **gross revenue up 11 percent** (Rp8,228 billion to Rp9,123 billion). The +53 percent is *net* segment revenue (Rp4,030,919 million to Rp6,175,190 million) from the 2023 annual report, Note 29 (Segment operations), checked in the local copy. **Fix:** cite the annual report for the revenue figure (and for the 60 percent incentive share in ch1:9, which is uncited), and say "net revenue". Keep the press release only for the GTV fall, or drop it.
3. **Katadata (2025) is dated 22 May 2026**, not 2025 (ch6:45). It is "Tim Publikasi Katadata" content that promotes a commercial partner (Everpro). On 2025 it supports only one change: a Rp1,250-per-transaction processing fee since July 2025. "Shopee raised the fees it charges sellers through 2025" is wider than that. **Fix:** re-key as Katadata (2026) or Medianti (2026). Narrow the sentence to the July 2025 processing fee, or find an independent report or Shopee's own seller notice. CNN Indonesia (2026) supports the 2026 rises by Shopee and TikTok Shop and the government's refusal to cap fees, but it does not mention Tokopedia. Katadata (2026) does ("Shopee, Tokopedia, dan TikTok Shop kompak mengubah skema biaya mereka").
4. **Trueman, Wong and Zhang (2000), "beyond revenue"** (ch3:179, ch5:118; the same idea in ch2:13). The paper (and Rajgopal et al. 2003, "over and above ... earnings and book value") shows web traffic priced beyond earnings and book value. It does not show pricing beyond revenue. Closest supportable wording: "markets priced measures of web activity beyond reported earnings".
5. **Ministry of Finance (2025) at ch4:131 points to the wrong document.** The VAT collected on foreign digital services (Rp5.51, 6.76 and 8.44 trillion for 2022 to 2024) comes from a DJP press release (`pajak.go.id/id/siaran-pers/pajak-atas-usaha-ekonomi-digital-terkini-3232-triliun`). It does not come from PMK 37/2025. Give it its own entry (Directorate General of Taxes, year to confirm). Use one name for the ministry: ch1:15 and ch2:27 say "Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Indonesia 2025", while ch4:131 says "Ministry of Finance 2025".
6. **BPS (2025) covers two publications**: *Statistik E-Commerce 2023* (released 30 Jan 2025; the 41 percent rise and the 821 thousand count rise) and *Statistik E-Commerce 2024* (released 28 Nov 2025; the 2024 value of about US$81 billion). In APA these are BPS (2025a) and (2025b). Each use needs the right letter (see table).
7. **Hummels and Klenow (2005) split export *levels* across countries, not export growth.** The abstract says "the extensive margin accounts for around 60 percent of the greater exports of larger economies". ch2:19 and ch3:132 say trade economists "split the growth of exports". Closest supportable wording: "Trade economists split a country's exports in the same way: more of each good, or more kinds of goods."
8. **Smaller wording points.** (a) ch2:29 Kleven: "income that employers reported" is narrower than the paper's "third-party reporting". Suggest "income that employers and banks reported". (b) ch3:124 cites Hagiu and Wright (2015) beside IFRS 15 for "records more of each sale". Hagiu and Wright support the marketplace-reseller distinction only; IFRS 15 carries the revenue claim, so move the Hagiu and Wright citation to the clause on business models. (c) ch2:9 "earns fees for arranging trades, not the value of each sale" is an accounting point, not a finding of Rochet and Tirole, Armstrong or Evans and Schmalensee. It is harmless as framing, but the IFRS 15 sentence that follows is its real support. (d) ch2:19 and ch3:5 describe Henderson et al. as testing a figure against the activity it stands for. The paper "use[s] satellite data on night lights to augment official income growth measures", and it does compare them, so the wording is acceptable.
9. **Uncited claims that need a source** (found while checking): ch2:7, "Investors and analysts often read revenue as a sign of how much commerce a platform carries" (already logged in EDIT_HISTORY as open); ch1:5, "The government then banned transactions on social media platforms, and TikTok Shop closed" (cite Permendag 31/2023 and a news report of the 4 Oct 2023 closure); ch6:37, Grab's 36 percent and "less than 6 percent" (placeholder already in the text); ch4:111 and ch5:208, the World Bank Enterprise Surveys, cited as data nowhere; ch4:67, LSEG; ch4:129, BPS national accounts.
10. **ch6:65 "Since 2024, e-commerce operators must send BPS..."** The regulation behind it (Peraturan BPS 4/2023) was enacted 9 March 2023 and in force on promulgation, 20 March 2023 (Art. 14), with a trial of up to six months (Art. 13). "Since 2023" is what the text supports, unless "2024" refers to a later enforcement step with its own source. The 61 and 75 operators (Republika 2024) are correct and refer to the fourth quarter of 2024.
