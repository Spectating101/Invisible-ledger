# Finance framing: sources and draft sentences (15 Oct 2026)

Purpose: give the thesis a deliberate finance frame before Prof. Thomas asks "where is the finance?". This file gathers checked finance and accounting sources, drafts sentences for 1.2, 2.1, 3.6, 5.4 and 7.2, and lists the questions a finance examiner could still ask. Nothing here has been inserted into a chapter. The researcher decides what goes in.

How the sources were checked (15 Oct 2026): Crossref metadata for every DOI (authors, year, title, journal, volume, issue, pages); the abstract read from Crossref, the SSRN version on Crossref, Semantic Scholar or IDEAS/RePEc. Abstracts only, no full texts. "Verified" below means the metadata and the abstract were both read. "Metadata only" means the work exists as cited but its finding was not read from an abstract, so it should not carry a claim until checked. Standards text was read from IASB and FASB staff papers that reproduce the paragraphs.

## 2. Draft sentences

Each sentence was counted at 13 to 18 words (citations excluded). Numbers in 7.2 repeat the headline from 5.2 to 5.4, as the conclusion may.

### (i) Section 1.2: a finance paragraph (could replace the first contribution paragraph)

> For investors, the thesis studies a measurement problem: which figure should carry a platform's growth? Market values of internet firms track revenue, and regulators worried about pressure to report high revenue (Bowen, Davis and Rajgopal 2002). Revenue growth, however, mixes two sources: more commerce and a larger share of each sale. Equation (5) separates them with two public figures, so an investor can check which one moved. When the take rate moves, a revenue multiple and a transaction-value multiple move by different amounts. In Indonesia, the share a platform keeps moved far more than at platforms abroad. The thesis also tests, in a prediction registered in advance, how market value moves with each source.

Source check: sentence 2 rests on Bowen et al.'s abstract ("verifies the association between market capitalization and revenue"; "concern about pressures on Internet firms to report high levels of revenue"). Sentence 5 is an identity: Δln(MV/R) − Δln(MV/V) = −Δln m (the independent review's point; it describes the denominator, not investors). Sentence 6 has no number, because its home is 5.2.

### (i) Section 7.2: the investor paragraph (could replace the current first paragraph)

> For investors, the thesis shows that a platform's revenue growth can differ sharply from its commerce growth. Equation (5) splits revenue growth into more commerce and a larger take rate with two public figures. In Indonesia, the take rate accounted for about two thirds of a revenue move, against a quarter abroad. The same split shows when a revenue multiple and a transaction-value multiple will disagree. Across 28 listed platforms, market value moved with transaction value, as the registered prediction stated. A 1 percent rise in transaction value went with about 0.6 percent more market value. The data do not show that markets paid less for growth from a higher take rate. Earlier studies of internet firms found that markets priced measures of web activity beyond earnings (Trueman, Wong and Zhang 2000; Rajgopal, Venkatachalam and Kotha 2003). Here the activity measure is transaction value, which the platforms in the sample report themselves.

Source check: two thirds and a quarter (5.3, `wedge_split.py`); 28 platforms, 0.6, p = 0.002 (5.4; `PREREGISTRATION.md` 8 Oct, b1 = 0.60); Trueman et al. (2000) and Rajgopal et al. (2003) abstracts say "incremental explanatory power" and "over and above ... earnings and book value". The last sentence is true of the M sample (whole-company transaction value from filings, `ch4` 4.5).

### (ii) Section 2.1: grounding "investors read revenue as a sign of commerce"

No source found says investors treat revenue as a measure of commerce in those words. The closest supportable version says that investors price revenue, and price it most where earnings say little. Pick one or two:

- At internet firms, share prices respond to revenue news beyond what earnings news shows (Davis 2002).
- Market values of internet firms track revenue, and regulators worried about pressure to report high revenue (Bowen, Davis and Rajgopal 2002).
- Revenue helps value firms beyond earnings, and it is more useful for technology firms (Chandra and Ro 2008).
- Where internet firms report losses, prices respond to revenue surprises and not to earnings surprises (Bagnoli, Kallapur and Watts 2001). *(Working paper; cite as such or drop.)*

Optional, to add the multiples point honestly:

- Among the multiples studied, a multiple of sales tracks share prices least closely (Liu, Nissim and Thomas 2002).

### (ii-b) Section 3.6: why the place of an incentive cut could matter (optional)

- Markets value a dollar of revenue surprise more than a dollar of expense surprise (Ertimur, Livnat and Martikainen 2003).
- Under IFRS 15, a cut in vouchers raises revenue, though it works like a cut in costs.

The second sentence is the author's reading of paragraph 70 and is marked as such. Read the Ertimur et al. abstract on IDEAS before using the first.

### (iii) Section 5.4: link to gross-versus-net research

- Earlier work found that revenue explained internet firms' prices less after 2000 where firms grossed it up (Davis 2002).

A possible follow-on, the author's own reading: "So the way a platform records revenue can change what revenue tells investors." (13 words)

## 3. What a finance examiner could still ask, and the answer in hand

1. **"Where is the finance?"** The measurement problem for valuation: revenue and transaction value give different growth rates and different multiples whenever the take rate moves (equations (4) and (5)). The investor check splits revenue growth with two public numbers (5.3). The registered market-value test (M2) holds: β_V ≈ 0.6, p = 0.002. Put this in 1.2 and 7.2 in those words (Section 2 above).
2. **"Why not stock returns or an event study?"** Market-value changes include share issues, so they are not returns (4.5, 7.4). The return test is planned in the proposal's Section 8 and listed in 7.5. M2 is stated as an association.
3. **"Your second prediction failed. So what did you learn?"** β_m is positive (about 0.3) but too uncertain to tell from zero; the gap is not significant (p = 0.20; 0.16 trimmed). The registration itself warned that a higher take rate also means higher margins, so a positive β_m is a plausible result (`PREREGISTRATION.md`, 8 Oct). Report both numbers, as 5.4 does.
4. **"Is the market sample about Indonesia?"** No. It has Bukalapak and 27 platforms abroad (4.5). The test asks how markets price the two sources of growth. The Indonesian part is that its take rates move far more (5.2), so the split matters more there. Say so once, in 4.5 or 7.4.
5. **"Sales multiples are the worst multiple. Why does revenue matter?"** Liu, Nissim and Thomas (2002) rank sales last, but their sample needs positive earnings and forecasts. Where earnings are negative, prices respond to revenue (Bagnoli et al. 2001, working paper; Chandra and Ro 2008: revenue matters more in extreme earnings cases). Many platforms in the sample made losses in some years.
6. **"Is transaction value reliable? It is not audited."** It is a company-defined KPI. The SEC (2020) asks for a definition and disclosure of method changes, and Givoly et al. (2019) find a KPI tells investors less when its computation changes. The thesis pairs only figures on one definition (Chapter 3 pairing rule) and labels every value. Point to these in 4.x.
7. **"Do the foreign platforms book incentives the same way?"** This is the open risk. The FASB's task force found "significant diversity in practice" on whether a marketplace's incentives to end users reduce revenue (EITF 2026), and the FASB added a project in August 2026. If some US platforms in the 27 record consumer incentives as marketing expense, their take rates would move less when incentives change. That would widen the Indonesia–abroad gap for an accounting reason. **Check before the defence:** read each foreign platform's revenue-recognition note on incentives (at least Uber, DoorDash, Lyft, Airbnb, Booking, eBay, Etsy, Mercado Libre). If most deduct them, the answer is ready. If several do not, state it once in 7.4.
8. **"Does a bigger take rate mean a better firm?"** Not in itself. The split shows how revenue grew, not whether value was created. Margins and cash flow are outside the tests. Keep the claim at "how revenue grew".

## 4. Corrections to existing text and notes (for the reference check)

- **3.6 and 5.4 wording.** Both chapters say Trueman, Wong and Zhang (2000) found that markets priced web activity "beyond revenue". The abstract does not mention revenue. It says web usage adds explanatory power beyond net income and its components (gross profit is priced). Rajgopal et al. (2003) and Hand (2001) say "beyond earnings". Closest supportable version: "Early studies of internet companies found that markets priced measures of web activity beyond earnings (Trueman, Wong and Zhang 2000; Rajgopal, Venkatachalam and Kotha 2003)." (14 words.) Whether revenue entered their model can be confirmed only from the full text.
- **2.1, "Discounts and vouchers that it pays for are normally deducted from its revenue (IASB 2014)."** Paragraph 70 covers payments to the customer and to "other parties that purchase the entity's goods or services from the customer". For a marketplace acting as agent, the merchant is the customer and the shopper is the merchant's customer. Whether vouchers to shoppers fall under paragraph 70 is a judgement, raised by the TRG in 2015 and still debated by the FASB in 2026. The working list's audit note already says to lead with the issuer's disclosed deduction (GoTo annual report 2023, Note 29). Keep the sentence, and support it with GoTo's note as well as the standard.
- **Davis (2002).** It is in *Journal of Accounting Research* 40(2), not *The Accounting Review*.
- **Tang (2025).** It is Tang and Huang (2025), *JBFA* 52(1), 569–608.
- **Hagiu and Wright (2015).** Check that the cited paper is the one 2.1 needs: "Marketplace or reseller?" (*Management Science*) or "Multi-sided platforms" (*IJIO*).
- **Liu, Nissim and Thomas (2002).** Verified, but not cited in any chapter. Add it only if the multiples sentence goes into 2.1.
- **Damodaran.** Replace the vague entry with Damodaran (2009), SSRN 10.2139/ssrn.1418687, if it is used at all.
- **IFRS 15 citation.** If paragraphs are cited, add IASB (2016) *Clarifications*, which amended B34–B38.
