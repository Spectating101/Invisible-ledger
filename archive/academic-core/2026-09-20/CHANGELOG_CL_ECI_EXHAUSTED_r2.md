## Core conceptual rebuild

- Replaced a fixed stage/pipeline interpretation with **claim-conditioned boundary invocation**.
- Defined the claim `C`, invoked-boundary set `I(C)`, passed-boundary set `P(C)`, and unresolved deficit `D(C) = I(C) \ P(C)`.
- Added four explicit propositions:
  1. **Conditional invocation** — a boundary is required only when the claim asserts the property governed by it.
  2. **Non-substitutability** — passing one invoked boundary cannot compensate for failure at another.
  3. **Claim-expansion monotonicity** — stronger claims cannot require less evidence.
  4. **Coupling condition** — claims depending on records from different systems require an explicit rule binding identity, quantity, time, location, and failure states across those records.
- Clarified that closure is **necessary, not proven sufficient**: an empty evidentiary deficit does not establish desirability, safety, adoption, or universal legal validity.

## Evidence updates inherited from the exhausted companion papers

- Integrated ECI's new **target-validity** result: Taiwan semiconductor electricity is physically direct and sector-specific but fails as a short-run annual production-growth proxy, including the 2023 sign reversal and the 2024-2025 rebound understatement.
- Recast CEIR as a **shortcut test**: real physical expenditure cannot bypass claim qualification or constraint closure.
- Replaced the older categorical attribution-versus-delivery claim with the **coupled-ledger correction**: renewable PPAs and Guarantees of Origin / EACs can be bundled, while remaining distinct records whose correspondence must be explicit.
- Added the corollary **granularity is not settlement**: tighter temporal matching improves evidentiary correspondence without itself creating a delivery or redemption right.
- Integrated CL's pricing result as a closure test: reproducible calculations do not earn binding authority when the model is mismatched to the physical process.
- Integrated the controlled workbench as execution evidence, while keeping operator-grade evidence, legal settlement, adversarial oracle testing, and bounded governance explicitly open.
